Spider plant care is simple: bright, indirect light, water once the top 2.5 cm (1 in) of compost has dried, and a room between 18 and 24°C (65 and 75°F). It tolerates the odd missed watering thanks to its thick, water-storing roots. The two things owners ask about most, brown tips and missing babies, have specific causes covered below.

## Spider plant care at a glance

| Need | What to give it |
|---|---|
| Light | Bright, indirect. Tolerates shade; no midday sun |
| Water | When the top 2.5 cm (1 in) is dry. Less often in winter |
| Humidity | Ordinary room air; very dry air browns the tips |
| Temperature | 18 to 24°C (65 to 75°F) by day, 10 to 13°C (50 to 55°F) at night is fine, above 7°C (45°F) |
| Soil | General-purpose houseplant compost, pot with drainage holes |
| Feeding | Water-soluble houseplant fertiliser at label rate or less, during active growth only |
| Toxicity | Non-toxic to cats and dogs |

The spider plant (*Chlorophytum comosum*) is native to a wide stretch of tropical and southern Africa, from West Africa to South Africa. It grows in clumps of arching, strap-shaped leaves from thick, fleshy roots that store water, and sends out long stems carrying small plantlets, the "babies" or "spiderettes".

## How much light does a spider plant need?

Bright, indirect light gives the fullest plant and the strongest variegation. [Clemson Extension](https://hgic.clemson.edu/factsheet/spider-plant/) notes that it tolerates some direct sun, but the midday sun scorches the leaves. [NC State Extension](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/chlorophytum-comosum/) adds that it grows in medium light and tolerates deep shade, which is why it survives in offices and hallways.

In low light it survives but slows down. Leaves go darker green, the stripes fade, and the plant rarely sends out runners. Sun scorch shows as pale, then brown, dry patches on leaves facing the window. Our [houseplant light guide](/en/blog/houseplant-light-guide) helps you read a window.

## How often should you water a spider plant?

Water when the top 2.5 cm (1 in) of compost has dried. Clemson recommends letting it dry out briefly between waterings. Water thoroughly until it drains, and empty the saucer. In a bright, warm room in spring and summer that is often once a week; in winter, every two to three weeks, as the plant grows more slowly and NC State advises reducing watering.

Spider plants forgive underwatering far better than overwatering. Their tuberous roots hold a reserve, so a plant that missed a watering usually looks pale and limp and recovers within a day. Clemson notes that poor drainage or overwatering causes root rot, which shows as yellowing leaves, a soft base and a sour smell from the compost. ## Why does my spider plant have brown tips?

Brown leaf tips are the most common spider plant complaint, and there are several causes:

1. **Tap water.** NC State notes that chlorine and fluoride in tap water can cause tip burn. Rainwater, or filtered water, helps if your plant browns despite good care.
2. **Salt build-up.** Clemson links tip burn to too much fertiliser or water high in soluble salts. A white crust on the compost or the pot rim is the sign. Flush the pot with plenty of water every few months and feed less.
3. **Dry compost.** Letting the pot dry out completely and staying dry for days.
4. **Dry air.** Clemson also lists low humidity, typically next to a radiator in winter.

Brown tips do not turn green again. Trim them off with clean scissors at an angle that follows the natural point of the leaf. New leaves show whether the change worked.

## Why isn't my spider plant making babies?

Spider plants make plantlets in response to day length. NC State explains that they develop under short days, meaning less than 12 hours of light a day for at least three weeks. Clemson notes that most offshoots appear as the days shorten in autumn.

That is why a spider plant under a lamp left on late into the evening, or in a room lit every night, may never send out runners. Some older articles recommend 12 to 16 hours of light to encourage babies, which is the opposite of what the plant needs.

Other reasons a plant may not produce runners:

- **It is young.** Small, recently potted plants probably need to fill their pot before they flower and set plantlets.
- **Too much fertiliser.** NC State notes that over-fertilising decreases plantlet formation.
- **Too little light overall.** A plant in deep shade survives but rarely runs.

## How to propagate spider plant babies

Spider plants are among the easiest houseplants to propagate. NC State lists plantlets and division.

1. Choose a plantlet with small roots or root bumps at its base.
2. Either pin it, still attached, onto a small pot of moist compost next to the mother plant and cut the runner once it has rooted, or cut it off and stand its base in a glass of water until the roots are a few centimetres long.
3. Pot it in ordinary houseplant compost and keep it lightly moist for the first few weeks.

## When to repot a spider plant

Spider plants grow thick, white, tuberous roots that fill a pot quickly. Clemson advises dividing or repotting before the roots crack the pot. Signs it is time: roots pushing out of the drainage holes, a pot that bulges or a plant that dries out a day or two after watering.

Repot in spring into a pot one size up, with general-purpose compost. To divide, pull or cut the root ball into two or three clumps, each with leaves and roots. Clemson recommends waiting four to six months before feeding a freshly repotted plant. Our guide to the [best soil for houseplants](/en/blog/best-soil-for-houseplants) explains what to look for in a compost.

## Spider plant care in winter

In winter, light and growth both drop. Water less often, only once the top of the compost is dry, and stop feeding until spring. Keep the plant out of cold draughts and away from radiators. Clemson notes that cool nights of 10 to 13°C (50 to 55°F) suit it, and NC State gives 7°C (45°F) as the lower limit. Short winter days are also when many plants start sending out runners.

## Common pests

Clemson lists whiteflies, spider mites, scale and aphids. Check under the leaves and along the runners when you water. Fine webbing and pale speckles point to spider mites, small white flying insects to whiteflies, and brown bumps along the leaves to scale. Each of these has its causes and fixes in our article on [spider plant problems](/en/blog/spider-plant-problems).

## Is a spider plant safe for cats?

Yes. The [ASPCA](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/animal-poison-control/toxic-and-non-toxic-plants/spider-plant) lists spider plant as non-toxic to cats and dogs. Cats often love chewing the dangling leaves, which does not poison them but can leave a ragged plant and the occasional upset stomach. Hang it out of reach if your cat treats it as a toy.
