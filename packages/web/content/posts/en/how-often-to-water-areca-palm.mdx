How often to water an areca palm depends on the season: in a bright, warm room that is usually about once a week from spring to late summer, and every two to three weeks in winter. The rule behind those numbers comes from [NC State Extension](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/dypsis-lutescens/): keep the compost moist but not soggy during the growing season. In practice, water when the top 2 to 3 cm (1 in) feels dry, and let it dry a little deeper in winter.

## How do you tell when an areca palm needs water?

Check the compost, not the calendar. Three checks work, and using two of them together is more reliable than either alone:

1. **The finger test.** Push a finger into the compost up to the second knuckle, about 2 to 3 cm (1 in). If it feels dry at that depth, water. If it still feels cool and damp, wait a day or two and check again.
2. **The weight of the pot.** Lift the pot right after a thorough watering, then again a few days later. A dry pot is noticeably lighter. After a few weeks you can tell by lifting alone. For a large floor plant, tilt the pot slightly instead.
3. **A moisture meter.** A cheap probe is useful in deep pots where your finger cannot reach the root zone. Water when it reads at the dry end of the "moist" range. Wipe it clean between plants.

The fronds also tell you, but later. Slightly limp fronds and leaflets that fold inward are early thirst signs. By the time tips go brown and crisp, the palm has been dry for a while.

## How often should you water an areca palm in each season?

These are starting points for a palm in a bright room in a pot with a drainage hole. Use them to decide when to check, and let the compost decide when to water.

| Season | Starting interval | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Spring | Every 7 to 10 days | Top 2 to 3 cm (1 in) dry |
| Summer | Every 5 to 7 days | Top 2 to 3 cm (1 in) dry; check more often in a heatwave |
| Autumn | Every 10 to 14 days | Top 3 to 5 cm (1 to 2 in) dry |
| Winter | Every 2 to 3 weeks | Top 5 cm (2 in) dry |

The winter gap is longer because growth slows with the short days. The palm uses less water, and compost that stays wet in a cool room is how root rot starts. Heating changes that a little: a palm next to a warm radiator in a dry flat may need water every 10 days even in January. The check, not the table, tells you.

## How should you water an areca palm?

Water thoroughly, then let it drain. Pour slowly over the whole surface of the compost until water runs out of the drainage hole. This wets the entire root ball and flushes out some of the salts left by fertiliser and tap water.

Empty the saucer or cachepot after 15 to 30 minutes. A palm standing in water keeps the bottom of the root ball soggy, and that is where rot starts.

Use water at room temperature. Cold water straight from the tap in winter is a shock for a tropical plant. If your tap water is very hard, or you see a white crust building up on the compost, use rainwater or filtered water when you can, and flush the pot with plenty of plain water every few months.

Avoid small, frequent sips. A glass of water every two days keeps the surface wet while the lower roots stay dry, which gives you the worst of both.

## What changes how often an areca palm needs water?

Four things shift the interval more than the season does:

- **Light.** A palm close to a bright window uses water much faster than one at the back of a room. NC State recommends bright, indirect light. If you move the palm, expect the interval to change too.
- **Pot.** Terracotta breathes and dries out faster than plastic or glazed ceramic. A small pot packed with roots can need water twice as often as a large one. A pot with no drainage hole holds water at the bottom, whatever the schedule. Our guide on [how to choose a pot](/en/blog/how-to-choose-a-pot-for-your-plant) explains the trade-offs.
- **Compost.** NC State recommends well-drained potting soil. A peaty mix that has broken down over the years holds water far longer than fresh, open compost. NC State suggests repotting every 2 or 3 years, which also replaces tired compost.
- **Temperature and air.** Warm, dry air from heating or a sunny window speeds drying. A cool room slows it right down. Areca palms like high humidity, and NC State suggests a humidifier if your air is dry. Humidity affects the fronds more than the roots, so do not try to fix dry air by watering more. Our guide on [how to increase humidity for houseplants](/en/blog/how-to-increase-humidity-for-houseplants) covers what works.

## What are the signs of overwatering an areca palm?

Overwatering is the more dangerous mistake, because it damages the roots before you see much above the compost.

- Several fronds yellowing at once, especially lower ones, while the compost stays damp
- Brown, soft patches rather than crisp tips
- Compost that smells sour or musty
- Stems that turn dark or soft at the base
- Fungus gnats hovering around the pot

If you see these, stop watering until the top 5 cm (2 in) is dry, empty any standing water, and check the roots. Healthy roots are firm and pale. Brown, mushy roots that smell bad are rotting. Our [areca palm problems guide](/en/blog/areca-palm-problems) goes through the diagnosis, and our [root rot recovery guide](/en/blog/root-rot-recovery) covers what to do if the roots are affected.

## What are the signs of underwatering an areca palm?

- Brown, crisp leaf tips spreading along the edges
- Fronds that droop, with leaflets folding inward
- Compost pulled away from the side of the pot
- A pot that feels very light
- Lower fronds yellowing and drying out quickly

Brown tips have more than one cause, so check the compost before you water more. Dry compost and a light pot confirm thirst. If the compost is dry all the way through, water slowly in two or three rounds a few minutes apart, so it soaks in rather than running down the sides. Cut off brown tips with clean scissors if you like. They will not turn green again. Our article on [overwatering vs underwatering](/en/blog/overwatering-vs-underwatering) compares the two sets of signs side by side.
