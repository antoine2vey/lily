Houseplant care in summer is the reverse of winter: plants grow fast, so check the soil every 2 to 3 days, water as soon as it is dry to the right depth, and feed every 2 to 4 weeks. Two things cause most summer damage: direct sun through hot glass, and pots that dry out completely during a heatwave or a holiday. If you move plants outdoors, wait until nights stay above 16°C (60°F) and give them about two weeks to adjust to the light.

## Watering in summer

Long days and warm rooms mean a plant uses far more water than it did in winter. A pothos that went three weeks between drinks in January may need water every 5 to 7 days in July. Small pots, terracotta and plants in bright spots dry fastest.

- **Check more often, don't water more often by default.** Test the soil every 2 to 3 days with a finger and water when it is dry to the depth your plant likes: the top 2.5 to 5 cm (1 to 2 in) for most foliage plants.
- **Water thoroughly** until water drains from the bottom, then empty the saucer. Plants left standing in water still rot, even in summer.
- **Water in the morning or evening** in a heatwave, when the room is cooler.
- **Succulents and cacti** grow in summer too. Water them when the soil is fully dry, which may be every 1 to 2 weeks rather than monthly.

## Feeding in summer

Summer is the main growing season, so it is when fertiliser does the most good. Feed actively growing plants every 2 to 4 weeks with a balanced liquid fertiliser at the label rate or weaker. Water first, then feed, so the fertiliser never goes onto dry roots. Skip a feed for any plant that is wilting, newly repotted or not putting out new leaves. Our guide on [how to fertilise houseplants](/en/blog/how-to-fertilize-houseplants) covers types and doses.

## Light: watch for sunburn through the window

The sun is high and strong in summer, and glass can magnify heat on leaves that touch it.

- Pull plants back 30 to 60 cm (1 to 2 ft) from south- and west-facing windows, or filter the light with a sheer curtain.
- Shade-lovers such as calatheas, ferns and peace lilies scorch first. Bleached patches or crispy brown areas on the side facing the window are sunburn.
- Succulents and cacti that tolerated a windowsill in spring can still burn if moved suddenly into stronger sun.

Our [houseplant light guide](/en/blog/houseplant-light-guide) explains how to read the light from each window.

## Heat and air conditioning

Most tropical houseplants are comfortable up to about 30°C (86°F) if they have water. Above that, they slow down and wilt in the afternoon.

- **Heatwaves:** move plants away from hot windows, water early in the day, and don't fertilise until the heat passes.
- **Air conditioning:** keep plants out of the direct flow of the vent. The cold, dry air browns leaf edges.
- **Afternoon wilting** that recovers by evening is heat stress, not thirst. Check the soil before you water.

Hot, dry air also favours spider mites. Check the undersides of leaves for fine speckling and webbing, and see our [spider mite treatment guide](/en/blog/spider-mites-treatment) if you find them.

## Moving houseplants outdoors for the summer

Many houseplants benefit from a summer outside, with stronger light and natural airflow. [University of Maryland Extension](https://extension.umd.edu/resource/moving-indoor-plants-outside) gives the rules:

1. **Wait for warm nights.** Move plants out only when night temperatures stay above 16°C (60°F), usually from mid-May to early June in temperate climates.
2. **Start in full shade.** Even sun-loving plants burn if moved straight into direct sun.
3. **Acclimate gradually.** Over about two weeks, move sun-lovers into more light a little at a time. Shade plants stay in shade all summer.
4. **Water and feed more often.** Wind and sun dry pots much faster outdoors. Small pots may need daily water in hot weather.
5. **Protect from storms.** Place pots where heavy rain can drain away and wind will not tip them over.

## Bringing plants back in

UMD advises bringing plants back indoors when nights fall below 16°C (60°F), often by mid-September. Before you do:

- Inspect leaves, stems and the soil surface for aphids, spider mites and scale.
- Treat with insecticidal soap or horticultural oil if you find any.
- Rinse the plant with a hose to knock off hitchhikers.
- Expect some leaf drop as the plant adjusts to lower indoor light.

## Going on holiday in summer

A summer holiday is the riskiest time for houseplants, because pots dry fastest. Before you leave:

- Water everything thoroughly the day before.
- Move plants away from sunny windows into the middle of a bright room, where they use less water.
- Group them together to raise local humidity.
- Use self-watering spikes, wicks or a tray of water for anything that dries in under a week.

## Houseplant care in summer: a checklist

- Check soil every 2 to 3 days and water when dry to the right depth.
- Feed every 2 to 4 weeks.
- Pull plants back from hot windows.
- Keep plants out of air-conditioning drafts.
- Inspect for spider mites weekly.
- Move plants outdoors only when nights stay above 16°C (60°F), and acclimate them over two weeks.
- Bring them back before nights drop below 16°C (60°F).
