How often to water basil depends mostly on the pot size and the sun, but the rule is the same everywhere: water when the top 2 cm (1 in) of soil feels dry, and never let the pot dry out completely. A small pot on a sunny summer windowsill may need water every day or two, a larger pot every 3 to 4 days, and indoor basil in winter every 4 to 7 days. Basil wilts fast when dry and rots when the soil stays soggy, so check before you water.

## How to tell when basil needs water

- **Touch the soil.** Push a finger in up to the first knuckle, about 2 cm (1 in). Dry at that depth means water now. Still damp means wait a day.
- **Lift the pot.** Small herb pots get dramatically lighter as they dry. After a week or two you will know the "needs water" weight by feel.
- **Watch the plant.** Basil droops within hours of running dry, and the youngest leaves at the top go limp first. It recovers within an hour or so of watering, but repeated wilting toughens the leaves and pushes the plant to flower sooner. Water before it wilts if you can.

[Penn State Extension's guide to growing herbs indoors](https://extension.psu.edu/growing-herbs-indoors) advises never letting the potting mix dry out completely. [University of Illinois Extension](https://extension.illinois.edu/herbs/basil) also says basil likes to be kept well watered. Neither means wet: the roots still need air, so let the surface dry between waterings.

## How often to water basil, by season and pot

These are starting points for basil in potting mix, in a pot with a drainage hole. Adjust them to what your finger test tells you.

| Situation | Spring and summer | Autumn and winter |
|---|---|---|
| Small pot (10 to 12 cm, 4 to 5 in) on a sunny windowsill | Every 1 to 2 days | Every 4 to 7 days |
| Medium pot (15 to 20 cm, 6 to 8 in) indoors | Every 2 to 4 days | Every 5 to 7 days |
| Large pot or window box outdoors | Every 1 to 3 days in hot weather | Not grown outdoors in cold months |
| Garden bed | A deep soak once or twice a week in dry spells | Not grown outdoors in cold months |

In winter, indoor basil grows slowly because the days are short. It uses much less water then, so the same pot that needed daily water in July may need it only once a week in January.

## What changes how often you water basil

**Pot size.** The single biggest factor. A small pot holds very little water, and a well-grown basil plant fills it with roots. If you are watering every day and the plant still wilts by evening, move it into a pot at least 15 cm (6 in) wide.

**Light.** Basil needs plenty of sun. Penn State recommends at least 6 hours of direct sun for indoor herbs, or grow lights for 14 to 16 hours a day. More light means faster growth and faster drying. A plant in a dim spot grows slowly, uses little water and is far more likely to rot if watered on a fixed schedule.

**Temperature.** Warm rooms and hot, sunny days dry the soil fast. Penn State says basil cannot survive below 10°C (50°F), so keep it off a cold windowsill in winter; cold, wet soil is the fastest way to rot the roots.

**Pot material and soil.** Terracotta dries much faster than plastic. Dense, peat-heavy compost holds water longer than a free-draining mix with added perlite.

**Outdoors.** Wind and full sun can dry a window box in a day, while a week of rain may mean no watering at all. [Lily](/en) adjusts outdoor watering for local rain and heat, and indoor watering for the season's daylight, so a basil pot's interval changes as conditions do.

## How to water basil

1. **Water the soil, not the leaves.** Wet leaves encourage basil downy mildew and other leaf diseases. Pour gently at the base of the stems.
2. **Water thoroughly.** Keep going until water runs out of the drainage hole. Small splashes only wet the top layer.
3. **Empty the saucer** after 10 minutes. A pot left standing in water rots the roots.
4. **Water in the morning** when you can, so any splashes dry during the day.
5. **Use room-temperature water.** Very cold water from the tap can chill the roots.

**Supermarket basil.** These pots hold dozens of seedlings crammed together. They dry out in hours on a warm sill, yet the dense root mass also rots easily. Split the clump into 3 or 4 smaller clumps and repot each into its own pot with fresh mix. They are far easier to water afterwards.

## Signs of overwatering basil

- Lower leaves turning yellow and soft.
- Soil that stays wet for days and smells sour.
- Stems darkening or going soft at the base.
- Drooping even though the soil is wet, which means the roots are rotting.
- Tiny black flies (fungus gnats) around the pot.

Let the soil dry more between waterings and check the roots. Our article on [basil problems](/en/blog/basil-problems) covers root rot and the other causes of yellow leaves, including downy mildew.

## Signs of underwatering basil

- The whole plant wilts, starting with the soft tips.
- Leaves feel thin and papery, and the edges may curl or brown.
- Soil pulls away from the edge of the pot.
- The plant can bolt into flower early.

Water thoroughly. If the soil has shrunk away from the pot, stand the pot in a bowl of water for 15 to 20 minutes so it rehydrates evenly. Our guide to [overwatering vs underwatering](/en/blog/overwatering-vs-underwatering) helps when the signs are mixed.
