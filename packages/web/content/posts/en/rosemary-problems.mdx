Most rosemary problems come back to one cause: roots that stay wet. [NC State Extension](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/salvia-rosmarinus/) calls overwatering the common cause of decline or death, and the RHS warns that cold, wet compost rots the roots, especially in winter. After that come dry indoor air, frost, too little sun and, less often, pests.

## Rosemary problems start with the pot and the sun

Rosemary is a Mediterranean shrub from dry, rocky ground. It wants full sun, at least 6 hours of direct light a day according to NC State, and soil that drains fast. Before you look at the leaves, check three things:

- **Drainage.** Does the pot have holes, and does water run out within seconds? Is it standing in a saucer of water or a cachepot without a hole?
- **Soil.** The [RHS](https://www.rhs.org.uk/herbs/rosemary/grow-your-own) recommends soil-based compost with up to 25% horticultural grit. Ordinary peat-heavy potting mix stays wet too long.
- **Light.** Indoors, only a sunny, south-facing window comes close to what it needs.

Most of the fixes below are one of these three.

## Why is my rosemary turning brown?

**1. Root rot from wet soil.** The most common cause. Needles turn brown or grey-brown, often starting at the base or on whole branches, while the soil is damp. The plant may look dry, which tempts you to water more. Tip it out of the pot: rotten roots are dark and soft and smell sour. If some white, firm roots remain, cut away the rotten ones, repot in fresh gritty mix in a pot with good holes, and water only when the top few centimetres are dry. If the stem is brown and soft at the base, take cuttings from healthy tips and start again. Our [root rot recovery guide](/en/blog/root-rot-recovery) has the steps.

**2. Dry indoor air in winter.** [Illinois Extension](https://extension.illinois.edu/herbs/rosemary) notes that indoor air dries rosemary quickly, usually leading to brown leaf tips and die-back, and that cool temperatures reduce the damage. Keep it in the coolest bright spot you have, well away from radiators. Do not answer it with more water: the same page warns that extra watering causes root rot.

**3. The pot dried out completely.** Rosemary is drought tolerant in the ground, but a small pot in hot, sunny weather can dry right through. The needles go dull, then brown and crisp, from the tips inward. Water thoroughly and check small pots more often in summer. The RHS notes that containers dry out quickly in hot, sunny weather.

**4. Frost or cold wind.** The RHS says established plants survive down to about -8°C (18°F), but harsh frosts and cold winds can damage shoots, and roots in pots can freeze. Browning on the exposed side after a cold spell is frost damage. Wait until spring, then cut back to green growth.

**5. Cutting into old wood.** If you pruned hard into bare, woody stems, those stems often brown off and do not regrow. The RHS advises against cutting into old wood.

## Why is my rosemary turning yellow?

**1. Wet roots.** Yellowing lower needles on a plant in damp soil are an early sign of the same problem as above. Let the soil dry and fix the drainage before it turns brown.

**2. Too little light.** Pale, yellowish new growth on long, thin stems means it is not getting enough sun. Move it to the sunniest spot you have, outdoors from spring to autumn if possible.

**3. Hunger.** A plant in the same pot for more than a year can run out of nutrients and turn pale overall. The RHS suggests a granular general-purpose feed in late spring or early summer for these plants, or repot it into fresh mix in spring.

## Why is my rosemary dying indoors in winter?

Rosemary is hard to keep indoors over winter. NC State says so plainly. The usual pattern is a plant that comes in healthy in autumn and slowly browns from the bottom up by February. Three things combine: too little light, warm dry air and soil that stays wet because the plant is barely growing.

What helps:

- **A cool, sunny spot.** Illinois Extension recommends a cool or even cold, sunny location. An unheated, bright room or a frost-free porch beats a warm living room.
- **Less water.** Keep the soil on the dry side. Check with a finger and water only when the top 2 to 3 cm (1 in) is dry, which in a cool room may be every two to three weeks.
- **Some humidity, plus air movement.** Illinois suggests a tray of wet pebbles under the pot. NC State warns that poor air circulation and high humidity cause powdery mildew, so do not crowd it among other plants.
- **Or leave it outside.** Illinois rates rosemary hardy only from USDA zone 8. In milder climates, including much of the UK and France, a potted rosemary often does better against a sheltered, sunny wall than indoors, with the pot wrapped and kept out of winter rain.

## What is the white powder on my rosemary?

Powdery mildew. It shows as a white, dusty coating on the needles, usually indoors or in a sheltered, still corner. NC State links it to poor circulation and high humidity. Snip off the worst shoots, give the plant more space and air, water the soil rather than the foliage, and move it into more sun. ## What pests attack rosemary?

NC State says rosemary is generally pest and disease free but suggests watching for aphids, mealybugs, whiteflies and spider mites, especially on plants indoors.

- **Spider mites.** Fine pale speckling and webbing at the shoot tips, worst indoors in warm, dry air. Rinse the plant in the shower and spray with insecticidal soap every 5 to 7 days for a few rounds. See our [spider mite treatment](/en/blog/spider-mites-treatment) guide.
- **Aphids and whiteflies.** Clusters on soft new shoots, or tiny white insects that fly up when you brush the plant, with sticky leaves below. A jet of water and insecticidal soap deal with both.
- **Mealybugs and scale.** White cottony tufts, or small waxy bumps on the stems. Dab them with a cotton swab dipped in rubbing alcohol and repeat weekly.
- **Rosemary beetle** (outdoors in Europe). According to the RHS, the adults are shiny, 6 to 7 mm long, with metallic green and purple stripes, and the grey larvae feed from late summer to spring. The RHS says the plant usually stays healthy and control is often unnecessary; pick them off or shake the branches over a sheet of newspaper.
- **Leafhoppers.** Coarse, pale mottling on the leaves in spring and summer outdoors. Usually cosmetic.
- **Cuckoo spit.** Blobs of white froth on the stems in late spring, made by froghopper nymphs. Harmless.

## Why is my rosemary woody and leggy?

Because it was not trimmed regularly. The RHS explains that unpruned plants become leggy, woody and bare at the base, and old, straggly plants rarely recover from hard pruning. Trim lightly every year after flowering, always into green, leafy growth. If the plant is already mostly bare wood, take cuttings from the soft tips in summer and replace it. Harvesting little and often also keeps it bushy.

## Quick answers

**Is rosemary safe for cats and dogs?** Yes. The ASPCA lists rosemary as non-toxic to dogs, cats and horses.

**Can a brown rosemary come back?** Brown needles do not turn green again. If you scratch a stem and find green underneath, that stem is alive and can regrow from its green parts. If the bark is brown and dry right down to the base, the plant is dead; start again from cuttings if any green tips remain.
