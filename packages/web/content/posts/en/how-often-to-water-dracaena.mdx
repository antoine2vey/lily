How often to water dracaena comes down to one check: water when the top 2.5 to 5 cm (1 to 2 in) of soil is dry. In a bright room that usually means every 1 to 2 weeks in spring and summer and every 3 to 4 weeks in winter. [NC State Extension](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/dracaena-fragrans/) puts it simply for the corn plant: keep the soil moist during the growing season and cut back on water in winter.

## When to water a dracaena

Push a finger into the soil. If the top 2.5 cm (1 in) is dry in a small pot, or the top 5 cm (2 in) in a large floor pot, it is time. If it still feels damp, wait a few days and check again.

Then water thoroughly. Pour slowly over the whole surface until water runs from the drainage hole, and empty the saucer after 15 minutes. A deep watering every so often is better than a small splash every few days, which keeps the top wet and leaves the bottom of the root ball dry.

Large dracaenas in heavy pots are hard to judge by eye. Two cheap habits help:

- **A wooden skewer.** Push it to the bottom of the pot and pull it out after a minute. Damp soil sticks to it and darkens the wood.
- **Weight.** Tip a small pot slightly just after watering and again a week later. A dry pot is noticeably lighter.

## How often to water dracaena in each season

These are starting points for a dracaena in a plastic pot near a bright window. They are not rules. Check the soil first and let it overrule the table.

| Season | Starting interval | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Spring | Every 1 to 2 weeks | Growth starts again as days lengthen |
| Summer | Every 7 to 10 days | Warmth and long days dry the soil fastest |
| Autumn | Every 2 to 3 weeks | Light drops and growth slows |
| Winter | Every 3 to 4 weeks | Little growth; soil stays damp much longer |

Most dracaenas get into trouble in autumn. The soil that dried in a week in July can stay wet for three weeks in November, but the weekly habit carries on. Check the soil before every watering through the first cool weeks and stretch the interval as you go.

## What changes how often a dracaena needs water

- **Light.** The biggest factor. NC State recommends bright to moderate, filtered light. A plant beside a bright window uses far more water than one in a dim corner, where the soil can stay wet for weeks.
- **Temperature.** NC State gives 21 to 27°C (70 to 80°F) as the range where the corn plant grows best, and advises keeping it above 10°C (50°F). A cool room in winter means slow drying and long gaps between waterings.
- **Pot and soil.** Terracotta dries faster than plastic or glazed ceramic. NC State asks for ordinary potting soil in a pot with drainage holes. A pot much bigger than the root ball holds wet soil the roots cannot reach. Our guide to the [best soil for houseplants](/en/blog/best-soil-for-houseplants) explains mixes.
- **Size of plant.** A tall cane in a pot it has filled with roots drinks quickly. A newly repotted plant needs less water until the roots grow into the new soil.

## How often to water different dracaena types

The common indoor dracaenas share the same rule but differ a little in how dry they like to get.

- **Corn plant (*Dracaena fragrans*), including 'Massangeana'.** Evenly moist in spring and summer, drier in winter, per NC State. Do not let it dry out completely in summer.
- **Dragon tree (*Dracaena marginata*).** NC State says to let it dry between waterings, but not severely. It handles a missed watering better than the corn plant.
- **Song of India and the other leafy types.** Treat them like the corn plant.
- **Lucky bamboo (*Dracaena sanderiana*).** Grown in water, it needs the water changed weekly, according to [NC State's lucky bamboo profile](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/dracaena-sanderiana/), not every two weeks as some guides say. Keep the roots covered. In soil, keep the mix moist.

## Does water quality matter for dracaena?

Yes, more than for most houseplants. NC State notes that dracaenas are sensitive to fluoride and to salts that build up in the soil, which show as brown leaf tips and edges. If your tips brown even though the watering rhythm is right:

1. Flush the pot once a month: run plain water through it at the sink for a minute and let it drain fully.
2. If your tap water is fluoridated, try rainwater or distilled water, as NC State suggests.
3. Feed lightly. Extra fertiliser adds to the salt load.

Dry air causes brown tips too. NC State says a humidifier can help in winter when the heating is on. Do not answer brown tips with more water, which only adds the risk of rot.

## Signs of overwatering a dracaena

- Lower leaves turning yellow and dropping, several at a time.
- Leaves yellowing from the base or going limp while the soil is wet.
- The cane softening or darkening at the soil line.
- Sour-smelling soil, or fungus gnats flying up when you water.

If the cane is still firm, let the soil dry out further than usual and check the drainage hole is clear. If the cane is soft at the base, unpot the plant and look at the roots: healthy ones are pale and firm, rotten ones dark and mushy. Trim the rotten parts and repot into fresh, barely damp mix. Our [root rot recovery guide](/en/blog/root-rot-recovery) has the full steps.

## Signs of underwatering a dracaena

- Leaves drooping and curling, with the soil pulled away from the sides of the pot.
- Brown, crisp edges spreading inward on many leaves at once.
- New leaves that come in small or stop altogether in summer.

Water slowly until it drains, wait a minute, and water again so the dry root ball takes it up. Brown edges will not turn green again; trim them with clean scissors following the leaf's shape. ## Quick answers

**Should I mist a dracaena?** Misting wets the leaves for a few minutes and does little for the room's humidity. If tips brown in winter, a humidifier does more.

**Is dracaena toxic to pets?** Yes. The [ASPCA](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/animal-poison-control/toxic-and-non-toxic-plants/dracaena) lists it as toxic to cats and dogs because of saponins, causing vomiting, drooling and loss of appetite, and dilated pupils in cats.

**Can I bottom-water a dracaena?** Yes. Stand the pot in a few centimetres of water for 20 to 30 minutes, then let it drain. Water from the top every few times to flush salts out.
