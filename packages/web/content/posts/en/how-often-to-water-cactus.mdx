How often to water cactus plants depends on the season more than anything else. From spring to early autumn, water thoroughly once the surface of the compost is dry, which in a sunny window is often every 1 to 2 weeks. From November to early March, a desert cactus rests and needs little or no water, just a light drink every few weeks if it starts to shrivel.

## When to water a cactus

The [RHS guide to houseplant cacti and succulents](https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/types/cacti-succulents/houseplants/growing-guide) gives a simple rule for the growing season: water thoroughly once the surface of the compost feels dry, let the excess drain away, and never leave the pot standing in water. It also advises against letting the compost dry out excessively between waterings while the plant is growing.

That second point surprises many people. Cacti survive drought, but they grow and flower best with regular water in summer. [Clemson Extension](https://hgic.clemson.edu/factsheet/indoor-cacti/) says that from March to September you can water a cactus like any other houseplant that likes to dry slightly between waterings. It calls watering the most important factor in growing cacti well.

Before each watering, check three things.

- **The surface.** Dry to the touch means it is time in the growing season.
- **The weight.** A dry pot feels much lighter than one you have just watered.
- **The plant.** A plump, firm cactus is fine. Slight wrinkling or shrinking in summer means it is thirsty.

## How often to water cactus by season

These are starting points for a typical desert cactus in gritty cactus compost, in a pot with drainage holes, on a sunny windowsill. They are estimates, not rules. Adjust them once you see how fast your pot dries.

| Season | Starting interval | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Spring (March to May) | Every 2 weeks, then weekly as it warms | Start again gently after the winter rest |
| Summer (June to August) | Every 1 to 2 weeks | Water thoroughly each time, once the surface is dry |
| Autumn (September to October) | Every 2 to 4 weeks | Space waterings out as light and warmth drop |
| Winter (November to early March) | None, or a light drink every 3 to 6 weeks | Only to stop the plant shrivelling |

The winter rest is the part most worth getting right. The RHS recommends keeping cacti cooler from November to the beginning of March, at night temperatures of 8 to 10°C (46 to 50°F), with minimal or no watering. Water only if needed to stop the plant shrivelling. Clemson gives the same advice, and says this usually means a light watering every few weeks. Both sources say the cool, dry rest helps cacti flower the following year.

In a warm living room, a cactus will keep growing slowly through winter and may need water a little more often than a plant in a cool room. Even so, keep winter watering light, as both sources advise.

## How to water a cactus

Water thoroughly. Pour water evenly over the compost until it runs out of the drainage holes, then let the pot drain. A sip that wets only the top centimetre does not reach the roots.

Empty the saucer or outer pot within half an hour. Clemson warns that waterlogged soil quickly rots cactus roots.

The RHS suggests tepid rainwater if you can collect it. Minerals in tap water can build up in the compost over time and leave white, chalky marks on the plants. Tap water is still better than none. Water at room temperature either way.

After repotting, Clemson recommends waiting several days before you water.

## What changes how often a cactus needs water

**Light and temperature.** A cactus in a hot, south-facing window dries out much faster than one in a cooler, dimmer spot. Clemson recommends a south- or west-facing window with several hours of direct sun.

**Pot material and size.** Terracotta dries faster than plastic or glazed ceramic, and a small pot faster than a large one. Clemson says cacti like to be slightly pot-bound and should move up only to a slightly larger pot, because extra compost stays moist and can cause rot.

**Compost.** A gritty, free-draining mix dries within days. Clemson suggests equal parts potting soil and very coarse sand or grit, or a ready-made cactus compost. Ordinary potting compost holds water for much longer, so the same watering routine can rot the roots. Our guide to the [best soil for houseplants](/en/blog/best-soil-for-houseplants) compares mixes.

**The type of cactus.** Forest cacti, such as the Christmas, Thanksgiving and Easter cacti, follow different rules. Clemson says they need much more water than desert cacti and should not dry out completely in spring and summer. The RHS notes that the Christmas cactus needs warmth and regular water in winter, when it flowers, and rests in summer instead.

## Signs of overwatering

- The base of the stem turns soft, brown or black.
- The cactus turns yellow and feels mushy rather than firm.
- It leans or topples because the roots underneath have rotted.
- The compost stays wet for a week or more after watering.

What to do: stop watering and check the roots. Healthy roots are firm and pale. Rotten ones are dark, soft and smell bad. Cut away the rot, let the plant dry, then repot into fresh, dry cactus compost and wait several days before watering. Our [root rot recovery guide](/en/blog/root-rot-recovery) and our guide to [cactus problems](/en/blog/cactus-problems) cover each step.

## Signs of underwatering

- The cactus looks wrinkled, puckered or shrunken.
- Its colour dulls and growth stops in summer.
- The compost has pulled away from the sides of the pot.

What to do: water thoroughly and let it drain. A thirsty cactus plumps up again once it has taken up the water. Some shrinking in winter is normal during the rest, so only give a light drink then.
