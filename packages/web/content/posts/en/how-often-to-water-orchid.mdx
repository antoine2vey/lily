How often to water an orchid depends mostly on what it grows in. A moth orchid (*Phalaenopsis*) in bark usually needs a thorough soak about once a week in spring and summer, and every 10 to 14 days in a cool room in winter. In sphagnum moss, which holds water longer, the gap is longer. Water when the roots and bark are nearly dry, not on a fixed day.

## How to tell when an orchid needs water

Check the plant, not the calendar. Three checks work, and the first is the most reliable.

1. **Root colour.** In a clear pot, look at the roots. The [University of Maryland Extension](https://www.extension.umd.edu/resource/care-phalaenopsis-orchids-moth-orchids) explains that roots turn from silvery white when dry to green when wet. Silver roots mean it is time. Green roots mean wait.
2. **Weight.** Lift the pot right after a watering and again a few days later. A dry pot of bark feels noticeably lighter. After a few weeks you can tell by lifting.
3. **A wooden skewer.** Push it into the middle of the pot and leave it for a minute. If it comes out dry and clean, water. If it is damp or darkened, wait a few days.

The surface of the bark dries first and tells you little. The roots in the centre of the pot stay wet longest, and those are the ones that rot.

The [Missouri Botanical Garden](https://www.missouribotanicalgarden.org/Portals/0/Kemper%20Gardens/Fact%20Sheets/Orchids%20-%20Phalaenopsis%20%282020%29.pdf) notes that moth orchids should never dry out completely. Unlike many orchids, they have no pseudobulbs to store water. Aim for nearly dry, then water.

## How often to water an orchid in each season

These are starting points for a mature moth orchid in medium-grade bark, indoors. Adjust them using the checks above.

| Season | Starting interval in bark | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| Spring | About every 7 days | New roots and leaves start growing |
| Summer | Every 5 to 7 days | Heat and long days dry the bark faster |
| Autumn | Every 7 to 10 days | Cooler nights slow growth; this is when flower spikes form |
| Winter | Every 10 to 14 days | Cool rooms and short days slow drying. Plants near a radiator may still need weekly water |

MoBot says a thorough watering once a week is usually enough for a mature plant in bark. UMD adds that at normal home temperatures a moth orchid should not need water more than once a week. Treat the summer interval as a maximum frequency, not a target.

In sphagnum moss, start at about double the bark interval and check with your finger. Moss should feel slightly damp, never soggy.

## What changes how often an orchid needs water?

- **Potting mix.** Bark drains fast and dries in days. Sphagnum moss holds water for much longer. Old bark that has broken down into fine crumbs holds water like moss, and is a common cause of rot. Fresh bark every one to two years keeps the interval predictable.
- **Pot.** Clear plastic pots with many holes dry evenly. Glazed ceramic cachepots trap moisture if water collects in the bottom.
- **Temperature and light.** A warm, bright windowsill dries the bark much faster than a cool, dim room.
- **Humidity.** In dry, heated air, bark dries faster. Raising humidity with a pebble tray, with the pot above the waterline, slows drying a little.
- **Plant size.** A plant with many roots in a small pot uses water faster than a young plant or one that has lost roots.

## How to water an orchid

1. Use lukewarm water, in the morning, so leaves and crown can dry by night.
2. Run water through the bark in the sink for about a minute, or drench it 3 or 4 times over 10 minutes, as UMD suggests. Bark repels water when it is very dry, so one quick splash runs straight through.
3. Let the pot drain fully. Never leave it standing in water in a cachepot or saucer.
4. Soak up any water left in the crown with a paper towel.

Skip the ice cubes. MoBot advises against them because they chill the roots and deliver too little water. Avoid water from a salt-based softener too. Our [orchid care guide](/en/blog/orchid-care-guide) covers light, feeding and repotting.

## Signs of overwatering and underwatering

| Sign | Overwatered | Underwatered |
|---|---|---|
| Roots | Brown, mushy, hollow; bark smells sour | Grey, thin, papery and dry |
| Leaves | Yellowing from the bottom, then soft; may wrinkle because rotten roots cannot drink | Limp, leathery and wrinkled; firm up after watering if roots are healthy |
| Mix | Damp days after watering; bark breaking down | Very light pot; bark dry right through |
| Flowers | Buds may drop | Buds may drop; flowers fade early |

Wrinkled leaves appear in both cases, which confuses many people. The roots settle it. Healthy firm roots with wrinkled leaves mean thirst. Mushy or hollow roots mean the plant is dehydrated because the roots have rotted, and more water makes it worse.

If you find rot, cut off the dead roots and repot into fresh bark.
