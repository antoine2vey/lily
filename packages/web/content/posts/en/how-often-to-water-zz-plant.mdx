How often to water a ZZ plant comes down to one rule: wait until the soil is completely dry, then water thoroughly. In practice that is about every 2 to 3 weeks in spring and summer and about once a month in winter. A ZZ stores water in the thick rhizomes under the soil, so it handles a missed watering far better than an extra one, and overwatering is what kills most of them.

## When to water a ZZ plant

Do not water by the calendar. Check the soil instead, and water only when it is dry all the way down.

- **Push a finger or a wooden skewer** deep into the pot. If it comes out clean and dry from the bottom half, the plant is ready. If any soil sticks to it or it feels cool and damp, wait.
- **Lift the pot.** A dry ZZ in its pot is noticeably lighter than one that was just watered.
- **Look at the drainage hole.** Soil there should feel dry before you water.

[NC State Extension](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/zamioculcas-zamiifolia/) advises letting the soil dry between waterings and avoiding wet feet. [Clemson Cooperative Extension](https://hgic.clemson.edu/factsheet/zz-plant-zamioculcas-zamiifolia-indoor-care-growing-tips-plant-guide/) goes further and recommends waiting until the soil is completely dry.

Older care advice sometimes recommends watering a ZZ on a consistent weekly schedule. That is the most common way to rot one. Its watering needs swing widely with light and season, so let the soil decide.

## How often to water a ZZ plant through the year

These starting intervals assume a plastic pot with a drainage hole, a free-draining mix and a room at normal indoor temperature. Use them as a first guess, then follow the soil.

| Season | Bright, indirect light | Low light |
|---|---|---|
| Spring and summer | Every 2 to 3 weeks | Every 3 to 4 weeks |
| Autumn and winter | About once a month | Every 5 to 8 weeks |

NC State gives a similar pattern: about once a month in winter, and twice a month in summer only when the soil is completely dry. Clemson describes watering 1 or 2 times a month once the soil is completely dry.

## What changes the interval

**Light.** NC State lists bright, indirect light as best, though the plant tolerates low light. In a bright spot it grows and drinks more. In a dim corner it may go well over a month between waterings, even in summer.

**Season.** Shorter, darker days in autumn and winter slow growth, and the soil stays wet much longer. Cut back as soon as you notice the soil taking longer to dry. [Lily](/en) adjusts indoor watering schedules for the season's daylight, so a ZZ's reminders spread out as winter comes.

**Pot and soil.** Terracotta dries much faster than plastic or glazed ceramic. Clemson suggests a mix of about 50% peat or coir-based potting soil, 25% perlite and 25% sand, which drains quickly. Dense compost on its own stays wet for weeks. For more on mixes, see our guide to the [best soil for houseplants](/en/blog/best-soil-for-houseplants).

**Pot size.** A small pot packed with rhizomes dries out faster than a large pot with plenty of loose soil. Very large pots for small plants are a common cause of rot.

**Temperature.** Clemson recommends keeping ZZ plants above 16°C (60°F). In a cold room, soil dries slowly and roots rot more easily, so water even less.

## How to water a ZZ plant

1. **Check the soil is dry** all the way down.
2. **Water slowly and thoroughly** until water runs out of the drainage hole.
3. **Let it drain completely.** Clemson advises letting the pot drain fully before putting it back into a decorative cachepot.
4. **Empty the saucer or cachepot** after 10 to 15 minutes. Never leave a ZZ standing in water.
5. **Wait until it is fully dry** before watering again.

Use room-temperature tap water. A ZZ is not fussy about water quality.

## Signs of overwatering a ZZ plant

Overwatering is the most common ZZ problem. Look for:

- Leaflets turning yellow, often starting with the lower ones on several stems at once.
- Stems going soft, dark or mushy at the base.
- Soil that smells sour and stays wet for more than a week.
- Rhizomes that feel soft instead of firm when you press the soil around them.

Stop watering and let the soil dry out completely. If stems are mushy at the base, take the plant out and check the rhizomes and roots. Healthy rhizomes are firm and pale inside; rotten ones are brown and soft. Cut away the rotten parts and repot into fresh, dry, free-draining mix. Our [root rot recovery](/en/blog/root-rot-recovery) guide walks through it.

## Signs of underwatering a ZZ plant

Underwatering is rare and easy to fix. Look for:

- Leaflets wrinkling, curling inward or losing their shine.
- Stems leaning or flopping after many weeks without water.
- Some leaflets yellowing and dropping in a very dry spell.
- Shrivelled rhizomes near the surface.

Give it a thorough watering. It usually recovers in a few days. If the soil has shrunk away from the pot, water slowly two or three times in a row so it soaks in evenly.

## Quick answers

**Should I mist a ZZ plant?** No. It does not need extra humidity, and misting does nothing for its watering needs.

**Is a ZZ plant safe for pets?** No. NC State lists medium toxicity for cats and dogs because of calcium oxalate crystals. Keep it out of reach.

**Can I bottom-water a ZZ?** Yes, as long as you let the pot drain completely afterwards and still wait for the soil to dry fully between waterings.
