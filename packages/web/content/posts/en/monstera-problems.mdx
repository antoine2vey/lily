Most monstera problems trace back to one of three causes: soil that stays wet for more than a week, light too dim for the plant's size, or air colder than 15°C (60°F). Yellow leaves are usually water, brown crispy edges are usually dry air or a skipped watering, and brown patches with yellow rims usually mean the roots are rotting. Find the symptom below, check the causes in the order listed, and apply the fix for the one that matches.

## Monstera problems at a glance

| Symptom | Most likely cause | Check first |
|---|---|---|
| Yellow lower leaves | Overwatering | Is the soil still wet 5 cm (2 in) down? |
| Brown, crispy edges | Underwatering, dry air | Is the pot light and the soil pulling away from the sides? |
| Brown patches with a yellow halo | Root rot or leaf spot | Do the roots smell sour? |
| Drooping leaves | Thirst or rot | Weight of the pot |
| Curling leaves | Thirst, heat or thrips | Soil moisture, then leaf undersides |
| New leaves without splits | Too little light or a young plant | Distance from the window |
| Long, bare stems | Too little light | Spacing between leaves |
| Silver streaks or speckles | Thrips or spider mites | Undersides of leaves with a torch |

For healthy-plant care, our [monstera care guide](/en/blog/monstera-care-guide) covers light, soil and repotting. The thresholds here come from [NC State Extension's Monstera deliciosa profile](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/monstera-deliciosa/) and the [University of Maryland Extension's watering guide](https://extension.umd.edu/resource/watering-indoor-plants).

## Why are my monstera leaves turning yellow?

Causes in order of frequency:

1. **Overwatering.** The most common reason by far. Yellowing starts on the lower, older leaves, often with soft stems and soil that stays damp for days. UMD Extension lists yellowing and leaf drop as the signs of too much water.
2. **Natural ageing.** One old leaf at the base yellowing while new leaves keep coming is normal. Cut it off once it is fully yellow.
3. **Too little light.** Pale, even yellowing across several leaves, slow growth and small new leaves.
4. **Underwatering.** Yellowing with crispy brown edges, on soil that is bone dry.
5. **Lack of nutrients.** Pale yellow between the veins on older leaves, on a plant that has not been fed or repotted in a year or more.

**How to tell them apart.** Push a finger 5 cm (2 in) into the soil. Wet means overwatering. Dry and crumbly means thirst. Neither, on a plant far from a window, points to light.

**The fix.** For overwatering, stop watering until the top quarter to third of the soil is dry, as NC State recommends, and make sure the pot drains. Empty the saucer every time. If yellowing keeps spreading after two weeks of drying out, check the roots for rot (see below). For a schedule by season, see [how often to water a monstera](/en/blog/how-often-to-water-monstera).

## Why does my monstera have brown edges or brown spots?

Look at the shape of the brown.

1. **Crispy brown edges and tips.** Dry air or soil that dried out completely between waterings. Common in winter with the heating on. NC State notes that monstera likes high humidity and suggests a humidifier or a tray of wet pebbles.
2. **Brown patches with a yellow halo, often in the middle of the leaf.** Usually root rot from overwatering. Less often, a fungal or bacterial leaf spot, which is more likely when leaves stay wet.
3. **Dry, pale or brown patches on the side facing the window.** Sunburn from direct sun. Move the plant back from the glass.
4. **Brown tips on many leaves with a white crust on the soil.** Salt build-up from fertiliser or hard water. UMD lists brown tips and a crust at the soil line as signs. Flush the pot with twice its volume of plain water.

Brown tissue does not turn green again. Trim crispy edges with clean scissors following the leaf's outline, and cut off leaves that are more than half damaged.

## Is my monstera overwatered?

Signs of an overwatered monstera, often appearing together:

- Lower leaves yellowing, sometimes with brown patches.
- Soil still wet a week after watering.
- Stems soft or dark near the soil.
- Droplets forming at leaf edges in the morning.
- Fungus gnats flying around the pot.

**The fix.** Stop watering. If the plant sits in a cachepot, take it out and let it drain. Move it to a brighter spot so it uses water faster. Wait until the top quarter to third of the soil is dry before watering again. If leaves keep yellowing or the stems are soft, unpot and check the roots.

## How do you save a monstera with root rot?

Slide the plant out of its pot. Healthy roots are firm and white to light brown. Rotting roots are dark, mushy and smell sour, and the outer layer slides off when you pull.

1. Wash off the old soil.
2. Cut away every soft root with clean scissors, back to firm tissue.
3. Repot into fresh, chunky aroid mix in a pot just large enough for the remaining roots, with a drainage hole.
4. Water once and leave it in bright, indirect light.
5. Do not water again until the top third of the soil is dry.

If most roots are gone, take stem cuttings with at least one node and an aerial root, and root them as new plants. The [root rot recovery guide](/en/blog/root-rot-recovery) covers both routes in detail.

## Why is my monstera drooping?

1. **Thirst.** Leaves droop and the pot feels light. Water thoroughly and the leaves lift within a day.
2. **Root rot.** Leaves droop while the soil is wet. Watering makes it worse. Check the roots.
3. **Transplant stress.** A droop in the week after repotting is normal. Keep it out of direct sun and do not fertilise.
4. **Cold.** NC State gives 60 to 85°F (15 to 29°C) as the monstera's range. A night by cold glass or an open door can leave leaves limp.
5. **Weak support.** Large stems lean and fold under their own weight. NC State recommends firm support so stems do not snap. A [moss pole](/en/blog/how-to-make-a-moss-pole) gives the plant something to climb.

## Why are my monstera leaves curling?

Leaves curl inward to save water. The usual causes are a dry pot, a hot spot near a radiator or in strong sun, or very dry air. If the soil is moist and the room is mild, look under the leaves: thrips and spider mites both cause curling and distorted new leaves.

## Why doesn't my monstera have split leaves?

Young monsteras make solid, heart-shaped leaves. Splits (fenestrations) come as the plant matures. A monstera that is a few years old but still makes solid leaves usually needs more light: move it to bright, indirect light close to a window. A plant that climbs a support also tends to make bigger, more split leaves than one that sprawls. New leaves that are smaller and less split than the previous ones are a light problem, not a disease.

## Why is my monstera leggy?

Long stretches of bare stem between leaves mean the plant is reaching for light. Move it to a brighter spot. You cannot shorten existing gaps, but you can cut a leggy stem back above a node in spring and root the top. Long aerial roots are normal. Tuck them into the pot or onto a moss pole, or trim them if they get in the way.

## What pests attack monstera?

NC State reports no significant pests but flags fern scale. In homes you will also meet:

- **Thrips:** silver streaks and tiny black dots on the upper surface, and twisted new leaves. See [how to get rid of thrips](/en/blog/how-to-get-rid-of-thrips).
- **Spider mites:** fine yellow speckling and webbing, worse in dry winter air.
- **Scale:** brown bumps on stems and leaf veins, with sticky residue below.
- **Mealybugs:** white cottony tufts in leaf joints.

Isolate the plant, rinse the leaves in the shower, and treat with insecticidal soap or horticultural oil, repeating as the label says.

## How to keep a monstera healthy

Almost every problem on this page is easier to prevent than fix, and most come back to watering. Water only once the top quarter to third of the soil is dry, give the plant bright, indirect light, and keep it away from cold glass in winter. [Lily](/en) sets a watering schedule for each plant and stretches it as indoor daylight falls in autumn and winter, which is when monsteras are most often overwatered.
