How often to water a jade plant depends on the season more than the calendar: in a bright room, most need water about every 2 to 3 weeks in spring and summer and every 4 to 6 weeks in winter. Those numbers are only a starting point. Water when the soil has dried out, then soak it, and give much less from autumn to late winter, as both [Clemson Cooperative Extension](https://hgic.clemson.edu/factsheet/jade-plant/) and [NC State Extension](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/crassula-ovata/) advise.

Crassula ovata is a succulent that stores water in its thick leaves and stems. That is why it forgives a missed watering and does not forgive soil that stays wet.

## How do you know when a jade plant needs water?

Check the soil, not the date.

- **Finger test.** Push a finger into the soil. In spring and summer, water when the top 2 to 3 cm (1 in) is dry. In winter, wait until the soil is dry most of the way down.
- **Weight.** Lift the pot just after watering and again when it is dry. A dry pot is noticeably lighter, and after a few rounds you can tell by feel.
- **A wooden skewer.** Push it to the bottom of the pot and pull it out. Damp soil clings to it; dry soil does not. This helps in deep pots where the top dries long before the roots.
- **The leaves.** A thirsty jade has leaves that feel slightly soft or look a little wrinkled, and they firm up within a day or two of watering. Do not wait for this sign every time, but it confirms the soil test.

Clemson's guidance is to keep the soil moist but not wet in spring and summer, and to let it dry between waterings in winter. In practice, for a jade in a pot with a drainage hole, that means letting the top layer dry in the growing season and the whole pot dry in winter.

## How often to water a jade plant in each season

| Season | Starting interval | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Spring (March to May) | Every 2 to 3 weeks | Growth restarts; intervals shorten as days lengthen |
| Summer (June to August) | Every 1 to 3 weeks | Small pots in a sunny window can dry in a week |
| Autumn (September to November) | Every 3 to 4 weeks | Stretch the gap as light fades |
| Winter (December to February) | Every 4 to 6 weeks | Let the pot dry fully; a cool room may need even less |

These intervals are rules of thumb for an indoor jade in a 15 to 20 cm (6 to 8 in) pot with a drainage hole, in a bright room. Your plant may differ by a week or more in either direction. Use the table to know when to start checking, and the soil to decide.

## How do you water a jade plant?

Water thoroughly, then leave it alone.

1. Pour water slowly over the whole soil surface until it runs out of the drainage hole.
2. Let the pot drain for 10 to 15 minutes.
3. Empty the saucer or cachepot. A jade standing in water for days is the most common way to start root rot.

Small sips every few days keep the top wet and the bottom dry, which is the opposite of what a succulent needs. A full soak followed by a dry spell matches how the plant stores water.

Ordinary tap water is fine for jade plants. Older posts often say to use distilled water or to let tap water sit overnight, but neither Clemson nor NC State mentions water quality as a concern for this plant. If your water is very hard and a white crust builds up on the soil, flush the pot with plenty of water now and then or use rainwater.

## What changes how often a jade plant needs water?

- **Light.** Clemson recommends 4 or more hours of direct sun. A jade in a sunny south or west window uses water much faster than one in a dim room, and a plant in low light can stay wet for weeks. Our [houseplant light guide](/en/blog/houseplant-light-guide) explains how to judge a window.
- **Pot.** Terracotta breathes and dries faster than plastic or glazed ceramic. A pot much bigger than the root ball holds water around the roots for days. Always use a pot with a drainage hole; see [how to choose a pot for your plant](/en/blog/how-to-choose-a-pot-for-your-plant).
- **Soil.** Clemson suggests a cactus mix with some organic matter. A dense, peaty mix stays wet far longer, and Clemson names slow-draining soil and frequent watering as the cause of root rot. Our guide to the [best soil for houseplants](/en/blog/best-soil-for-houseplants) covers gritty mixes.
- **Temperature.** Clemson gives 18 to 24 °C (65 to 75°F) by day and 10 to 13 °C (50 to 55°F) at night as the ideal range. A cool winter room slows drying a lot; a warm room next to a radiator speeds it up.
- **Plant size.** A large, old jade with a thick trunk holds more water and can go longer than a young cutting in a small pot.

## Signs of overwatering a jade plant

- Leaves turn yellow, soft or translucent, often starting at the bottom.
- Leaves drop even though they look plump. NC State lists leaf fall and root rot as results of overwatering.
- Stems feel soft or go dark at the soil line.
- The soil stays wet a week after watering and may smell sour.

If you see these, stop watering, take the plant out of the pot, and look at the roots. Healthy roots are pale and firm; rotten ones are brown and mushy. Our [jade plant problems](/en/blog/jade-plant-problems) page goes through each symptom.

## Signs of underwatering a jade plant

- Leaves look thin, wrinkled or shrivelled and feel soft but not mushy.
- Older leaves dry out and drop. Clemson notes that drought causes leaf spotting and drop.
- The soil is bone dry and has pulled away from the edge of the pot.

An underwatered jade recovers quickly. Soak the pot as described above and the leaves should plump up within a few days. If the soil has shrunk so much that water runs straight down the sides, stand the pot in a bowl of water for 20 to 30 minutes so the root ball can rewet.

When in doubt, wait. A jade plant survives a few extra dry days far better than a few extra wet ones. [Lily](/en) can set a watering schedule for each plant and stretch it automatically as winter days get shorter.

## Quick answers

**Should I mist a jade plant?** No. It does not need extra humidity, and wet leaves are no benefit to a succulent.

**Is a jade plant toxic to pets?** Yes. The [ASPCA](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/animal-poison-control/toxic-and-non-toxic-plants/jade-plant) lists it as toxic to cats and dogs, with vomiting among the signs.
