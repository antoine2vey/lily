String of pearls care is succulent care with one twist: the plant dislikes both soggy soil and long droughts. Give it bright, indirect light, a gritty cactus mix in a pot with a drainage hole, and water when the top 2 to 3 cm (1 in) of mix is dry. Keep it at 21 to 27°C (70 to 80°F) in summer and somewhere cooler, 10 to 16°C (50 to 60°F), in winter, with much less water.

## String of pearls care at a glance

| Need | What it wants |
|---|---|
| Light | Bright, indirect. Harsh direct sun scorches the pearls |
| Water | When the top 2 to 3 cm (1 in) is dry in summer; far less in a cool winter. Never left soggy |
| Humidity | Ordinary dry indoor air. No humidifier or misting needed |
| Temperature | 21 to 27°C (70 to 80°F) in summer, 10 to 16°C (50 to 60°F) in winter. No frost |
| Soil | Cactus mix, or potting mix with sand or perlite added |
| Toxicity to pets | Toxic to people, cats and dogs if eaten; sap can irritate skin |

String of pearls (*Curio rowleyanus*, long sold as *Senecio rowleyanus*) grows in dry parts of South Africa, creeping along the ground under shrubs and rocks and rooting where its stems touch soil. Most of the figures here come from the [NC State Extension plant page](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/curio-rowleyanus/). That page also warns that the plant is "very particular": it tolerates neither overwatering, underwatering nor poor light.

## How much light does string of pearls need?

Indoors, NC State recommends bright, indirect light and notes that direct sunlight can scorch the leaves. In practice that means close to an east window, or set back from a south or west window, or behind a sheer curtain. A few hours of soft morning sun is usually fine.

Some of the older posts on this site called a north window ideal. For most homes it is too dark. In low light the stems stretch, the gaps between pearls grow, and the plant thins out from the top. If your plant looks sparse near the soil and long at the ends, more light is the first fix. Our [houseplant light guide](/en/blog/houseplant-light-guide) helps you judge a spot.

Each pearl has a dark, translucent stripe along one side. NC State describes it as an epidermal window that lets light into the leaf, which is one reason the plant does poorly away from a window.

## How often should you water string of pearls?

In spring and summer, water when the top 2 to 3 cm (1 in) of mix is dry. Water until it drains from the hole, and empty the saucer. In a bright room in summer that is often every 7 to 14 days, but the mix, pot and light decide.

String of pearls needs a different balance from most succulents. NC State says it tolerates infrequent watering and that overwatering leads to root rot, but also says never to let the plant dry out completely between waterings. The [RHS](https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/types/cacti-succulents) puts the same balance for succulents in general: too much water leads to rot, too little causes shrivelling.

In winter, when the plant is kept at 10 to 16°C (50 to 60°F), it uses far less water. Water lightly, every three to four weeks or when the pearls start to lose their plumpness. In a warm, heated room it keeps growing and needs water more often than that.

How to read the pearls:

- **Thirsty:** pearls look deflated, wrinkled or slightly flattened, and the mix is dry right through. They plump up within a day or two of watering.
- **Too wet:** pearls turn soft, translucent or yellow, stems go brown or mushy at the soil line, and the mix stays damp for more than a week. This is root rot starting. See our [root rot recovery guide](/en/blog/root-rot-recovery).

NC State notes that wilting can come from either cause, so always check the mix before you water a shrivelled plant.

## What soil and pot does string of pearls need?

NC State recommends cactus potting mix, or ordinary potting soil mixed with sand or perlite. Aim for something that drains within seconds and dries within a week in summer. A simple homemade version is two parts potting mix to one part perlite or coarse grit.

Use a shallow pot with a drainage hole. Terracotta dries faster than plastic, which helps if you tend to overwater. The stems root along the soil surface, so a wide pot gives the plant room to fill out at the top. Our guides to [choosing a pot](/en/blog/how-to-choose-a-pot-for-your-plant) and to [soil for houseplants](/en/blog/best-soil-for-houseplants) go further.

## Does string of pearls need humidity?

No. It comes from dry country and does well in ordinary indoor air. Some older articles here recommended a humidifier or even a terrarium. Both keep the pearls and the soil surface damp, which encourages rot. Skip misting too.

## Why is my string of pearls thin on top?

A thin crown is common and has three usual causes:

1. **Too little light.** The top of the plant is often the darkest part of a hanging basket. Move it closer to the window.
2. **Rot at the soil line.** If stems near the soil are brown and soft, the mix has stayed wet. Remove the damaged stems and let the mix dry further between waterings.
3. **Age.** NC State notes that string of pearls tends to die back after several years and suggests starting fresh from cuttings rather than trying to revive an old plant.

To fill a thin pot, lay a few healthy strands across the soil surface and pin them down lightly. The nodes in contact with the mix will root.

## How do you propagate string of pearls?

NC State recommends stem cuttings.

1. Cut a strand 8 to 13 cm (3 to 5 in) long.
2. Remove the pearls from the bottom 2 to 3 cm (1 in) so a few nodes are bare.
3. Press those nodes into slightly damp cactus mix, or lay the whole strand flat on the surface and pin it down.
4. Keep the pot in bright, indirect light and water lightly when the surface dries.

NC State also mentions dropping single pearls on the mix, a slower and less reliable method. Our guide to [plant propagation from cuttings](/en/blog/plant-propagation-cuttings) covers rooting in more detail.

## Is string of pearls toxic to pets?

Yes. NC State describes the plant as toxic to both people and pets. Eating the leaves can cause vomiting and diarrhoea, and the sap can cause skin irritation and a rash. Hang it out of reach of pets and children, and wear gloves when you handle it a lot.

## Pests and other problems

NC State lists aphids, whiteflies, fungus gnats, ants and mealybugs. Mealybugs are the most likely indoors: look for white cottony tufts between the pearls. Remove them with a cotton swab dipped in rubbing alcohol. Yellow or grey pearls without signs of rot may point to insects, so check closely with a magnifying glass. Our article on [string of pearls problems](/en/blog/string-of-pearls-problems) covers each symptom in turn.

String of pearls needs little feeding. If you feed, use a balanced fertiliser at half strength a few times in spring and summer, and none in winter.
