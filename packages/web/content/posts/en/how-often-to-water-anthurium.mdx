How often to water an anthurium is set by the top of the mix: water when the top 2.5 cm (1 in) feels dry. In a warm, bright room that usually means every 5 to 10 days in spring and summer and every 2 to 3 weeks in winter. Anthuriums like their mix evenly moist but never soggy, and wet roots are the most common way they die.

## How do you know when an anthurium needs water?

[NC State Extension](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/anthurium-andraeanum/) recommends watering the flamingo flower (*Anthurium andraeanum*) when the top inch of soil feels dry, in a moist but well-drained mix. In the wild it grows as an epiphyte in the rainforests of Colombia and Ecuador, rooted in moss and bark on trees, where its roots get water often but are never left sitting in it.

Check before every watering:

1. **Push a finger into the mix up to the first knuckle**, about 2.5 cm (1 in). If it is dry at that depth, water. If it is still cool and damp, wait two or three days and check again.
2. **Use a chopstick in deep pots.** [University of Vermont Extension](https://www.uvm.edu/extension/news/houseplant-care-during-winter-months) suggests pushing a wooden chopstick into the mix: if soil sticks to it, the mix is still moist.
3. **Lift the pot.** After a few waterings you will feel the difference between a just-watered pot and one that is ready for more.

When it is time, water slowly over the whole surface until water runs from the drainage hole. Let it drain, then empty the saucer or cachepot. Water at room temperature is kinder to a tropical plant than cold water straight from the tap.

## How often to water an anthurium in each season

These are starting points for an anthurium in bright, indirect light in a heated home. Check the mix and adjust.

| Season | Starting interval |
|---|---|
| Spring | Every 7 to 10 days as growth picks up |
| Summer | Every 5 to 7 days in a warm, bright room |
| Autumn | Every 10 to 14 days as light falls |
| Winter | Every 2 to 3 weeks, by the finger test |

In winter, short days slow growth and the mix stays damp much longer. University of Vermont Extension notes that winter houseplant care starts with reducing water, and that we tend to give too much at that time of year. An anthurium that drank every week in July may need water only every two or three weeks in January, even though heating makes the room feel dry.

## What changes how often an anthurium needs water?

- **Light.** NC State recommends bright, indirect light and warns that direct sun can scorch the leaves. More light means faster growth and more frequent watering. In a dim corner, the plant uses little water and the mix stays wet for a long time.
- **Temperature.** NC State gives 18 to 27°C (65 to 80°F) as the preferred range and notes that anthuriums do not tolerate cold drafts or sudden temperature changes. A pot on a cold windowsill dries slowly; one near a radiator dries fast.
- **Pot and mix.** An epiphyte's roots need air as well as water. A chunky mix with orchid bark and perlite drains in seconds and lets you water generously without rot. A dense, peaty mix stays wet for a week or more. Use a pot with a drainage hole and only slightly larger than the root ball. Our guide to the [best soil for houseplants](/en/blog/best-soil-for-houseplants) explains how to make an airy mix.
- **Humidity.** NC State gives 60 to 80% as the preferred humidity. Dry air does not change how often the roots need water much, but it browns leaf tips and edges, which is often mistaken for thirst. Raise the humidity instead of watering more; our guide on [how to increase humidity for houseplants](/en/blog/how-to-increase-humidity-for-houseplants) covers the methods that work.

## What are the signs of an overwatered anthurium?

NC State lists overwatering as a cause of root rot, and yellowing leaves as a sign of overwatering or poor drainage, as well as of nutrient shortage.

Look for:

- lower leaves turning yellow, one after another, while the mix is still damp;
- leaves drooping or wilting even though the mix is wet, because rotting roots cannot take up water;
- brown or black patches spreading from the base of the stems;
- a sour smell from the pot, and roots that are brown and mushy instead of firm and pale;
- fungus gnats around the pot, a sign the mix is staying wet too long.

What to do:

1. Stop watering and let the top 2.5 to 5 cm (1 to 2 in) dry out.
2. If the plant keeps declining, take it out of its pot. Cut away soft, dark roots with clean scissors.
3. Repot in fresh, airy mix with plenty of bark, in a pot with a drainage hole that fits the remaining roots.
4. Water lightly, then wait until the top of the mix is dry before watering again.

Our [root rot recovery guide](/en/blog/root-rot-recovery) walks through severe cases.

## What are the signs of an underwatered anthurium?

Underwatering is less common indoors but happens in summer or in very airy mixes. Look for:

- leaves that droop or curl while the mix is dry and the pot feels light;
- crisp brown edges or tips spreading across older leaves;
- mix shrinking away from the side of the pot, so water runs straight through.

Water slowly until it drains. If the mix has dried into a hard block, stand the pot in a bowl of water for 15 to 20 minutes so the root ball soaks up evenly. The leaves should firm up within a day. Brown edges will not turn green again, but new leaves should come in clean.

If you are unsure which problem you have, the soil is the deciding clue: drooping in wet mix points to the roots, drooping in dry mix points to thirst. Our guide to [overwatering vs underwatering](/en/blog/overwatering-vs-underwatering) compares the signs side by side.

## Quick answers

**Should you water an anthurium with ice cubes?** No. Ice cubes deliver little water to one spot, chill tropical roots and leave the rest of the root ball dry. Water slowly with room-temperature water until it drains.

**Should you mist an anthurium instead of watering?** Misting wets the leaves for a few minutes and does not water the roots or raise room humidity for long. Water the mix, and use a humidifier or grouping if the air is dry.
