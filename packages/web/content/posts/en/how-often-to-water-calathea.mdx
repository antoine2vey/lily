How often to water calathea depends on keeping the soil evenly moist without letting it go soggy: water when the top 2.5 cm (1 in) has just dried, which in a warm room often means every 5 to 7 days in summer and every 10 to 14 days in winter. Use rainwater, distilled or filtered water at room temperature, and never let the pot stand in water. Curling leaves usually mean the plant went too dry or the air is too dry. Yellowing with wet soil means too much water.

## How to tell when a calathea needs water

Calatheas sit in an awkward middle. They dislike drying out, and they dislike sitting wet. The [NC State Extension](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/goeppertia-roseopicta/) entry for the rose-painted calathea asks for a moist, well-drained, peaty mix that is moist but not soggy or wet, and says overwatering can cause root rot. It also lists inadequate watering as a cause of curling leaves.

That leaves a narrow window, so check often.

- **Finger test.** [Clemson Extension](https://hgic.clemson.edu/factsheet/indoor-plants-watering/) calls touch the most accurate gauge. Push a finger into the mix to the first joint, about 2.5 cm (1 in). For a calathea, water as soon as it feels dry there. Do not wait for the whole pot to dry.
- **Pot weight.** A pot that suddenly feels light is ready. Learn the weight right after watering as a reference.
- **Leaves.** Leaves that start to curl inward along their length are often the first sign of thirst. Water then, and check sooner next time.

## How often to water calathea through the year

These starting points assume a plastic pot with a drainage hole, a peaty mix and bright indirect light. Check with your finger and adjust.

| Season | Typical interval | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Spring | Every 6 to 8 days | New leaves unfurl. Water as soon as the top dries. |
| Summer | Every 5 to 7 days | Warm rooms dry the mix fastest. Check twice a week. |
| Autumn | Every 7 to 10 days | Growth slows. |
| Winter | Every 10 to 14 days | Less growth, but heating dries the air, so watch the leaves. |

Clemson notes that plants need less water while they rest. In winter, a calathea near a radiator can still dry out fast, while one in a cool room can stay wet for two weeks. That is why the finger test matters more than the table.

## What changes how often you water

**Light.** NC State recommends bright, indirect light. Direct sun scorches the leaves, and too little light fades their colour. A plant in a brighter spot dries faster than one in shade.

**Pot.** Clemson explains that clay pots lose water through their sides and need watering more often than plastic or glazed pots. A small, root-filled pot dries faster than a roomy one. Plastic holds moisture longer, which suits a plant that likes to stay evenly moist.

**Soil.** NC State suggests a peaty mix with vermiculite or perlite added for drainage. A dense mix that stays wet invites rot. One that is too coarse dries out between checks.

**Temperature and humidity.** NC State gives 18 to 24 °C (65 to 75 °F) and humidity above 60%. Warmth speeds drying. Dry air does not change how often the soil needs water as much as it browns and curls the leaves. The [humidity guide](/en/blog/how-to-increase-humidity-for-houseplants) covers pebble trays, grouping and humidifiers.

## Which water to use for a calathea

NC State recommends rainwater or distilled water and advises against tap water, saying fluoride in it will turn the foliage brown. It lists fluoride in tap water, alongside low humidity, as a cause of brown leaves and tips. Filtered water is a common middle ground.

Use it at room temperature. Clemson suggests 17 to 22 °C (62 to 72 °F) and advises against softened water, which adds sodium to the mix.

Water the soil, not the leaves. NC State notes that wetting the leaves while watering, or poor air circulation, can lead to leaf spot. Pour slowly until water runs from the drainage hole, wait a few minutes, then empty the saucer.

## Signs of overwatering

- **Yellowing lower leaves with soil that is still wet.** The commonest sign.
- **Drooping in wet soil.** Clemson warns that roots damaged by rot cannot take up water, so the plant wilts, and watering more makes it worse.
- **Dark, soft stems at the soil line** and a sour smell from the pot. NC State lists root rot from overwatering among the main problems.

Stop watering until the top 2.5 cm (1 in) is dry. If the plant keeps drooping, check the roots. Firm, pale roots are healthy. Brown, mushy ones need cutting out and a repot into fresh mix. The [root rot recovery guide](/en/blog/root-rot-recovery) has the steps.

## Signs of underwatering

- **Leaves curl inward** and stay curled during the day.
- **Crisp brown edges** spreading inward.
- **Soil pulling away from the pot**, and a pot that feels light.

Water slowly until it drains, and check again in two or three days. If the mix has dried so hard that water runs straight through, Clemson suggests watering twice, half an hour apart, or setting the pot in a basin until the surface is moist. Brown edges will not turn green again, but new leaves will come in clean. The [overwatering vs underwatering guide](/en/blog/overwatering-vs-underwatering) compares the two.

## Are curling leaves always a watering problem?

No. NC State gives two causes for curling, low humidity and inadequate watering. If the soil is moist and leaves still curl, the air is the likely cause. Calatheas also fold their leaves up in the evening and open them in the morning, a normal movement shared by the prayer plant family. Only curling that stays through the day is a sign of stress. The [calathea problems page](/en/blog/calathea-problems) covers other symptoms.

[Lily](/en) schedules watering per plant and adjusts indoor intervals for daylight, so a calathea gets checked more often in summer than in winter.

## Quick answers

**Is a calathea safe for pets?** Yes. The ASPCA lists calathea as [non-toxic to cats and dogs](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/animal-poison-control/toxic-and-non-toxic-plants/calathea).

**Can I bottom-water a calathea?** Yes. Set the pot in a basin of water until the surface feels moist, then let it drain fully.
