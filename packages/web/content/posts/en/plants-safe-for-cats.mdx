The plants safe for cats below are all listed as non-toxic to cats by the [ASPCA](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/animal-poison-control/cats-plant-list), the American animal welfare charity that runs a 24-hour animal poison control centre. They include spider plant, Boston fern, calathea, moth orchid, African violet, peperomia and several palms. "Non-toxic" means the plant is not expected to poison your cat. It does not mean your cat can eat it freely. Any plant can upset a cat's stomach if it eats enough, so a cat that keeps chewing one plant still needs it moved out of reach.

## Plants safe for cats, sorted by light

Pick the plants that suit your windows. A plant in the wrong light struggles, and a struggling plant drops leaves your cat will play with.

### For bright, indirect light

| Plant | Botanical name | Why it suits a cat home |
|---|---|---|
| Spider plant | *Chlorophytum comosum* | Hardy, and it recovers quickly from chewed leaves |
| Boston fern | *Nephrolepis exaltata* | Looks good hanging, out of a cat's reach |
| Areca palm | *Dypsis lutescens* | A large floor plant to replace a toxic palm |
| Kentia palm | *Howea forsteriana* | Tall, slow and tolerant of indoor conditions |
| Moth orchid | *Phalaenopsis* | Flowers for months; keep the stake and clip out of reach |
| African violet | *Saintpaulia* | Small enough for a high windowsill |
| Bromeliads | *Bromeliaceae* | Many shapes and colours |
| Money tree | *Pachira aquatica* | A safe choice for a small indoor tree |
| Hoya | *Hoya carnosa* | Trailing or climbing, with scented flowers |
| Staghorn fern | *Platycerium* | Usually mounted on a wall, well out of reach |

### For medium or lower light

| Plant | Botanical name | Why it suits a cat home |
|---|---|---|
| Calathea and prayer plant | *Calathea*, *Maranta* | Patterned leaves, safe to chew |
| Parlour palm | *Chamaedorea elegans* | Copes with a room away from the window |
| Peperomia | *Peperomia* | Small, many leaf shapes, easy to place up high |
| Cast iron plant | *Aspidistra elatior* | Survives dim corners and irregular care |
| Polka dot plant | *Hypoestes phyllostachya* | Bright spotted leaves |
| Nerve plant | *Fittonia* | Small, suits a terrarium a cat cannot reach |

### For sun and a dry windowsill

| Plant | Botanical name | Why it suits a cat home |
|---|---|---|
| Echeveria | *Echeveria* | A safe rosette succulent, unlike jade or aloe vera |
| Haworthia | *Haworthia* | A small succulent for a sunny sill |
| Christmas cactus | *Schlumbergera* | No sharp spines, flowers in winter |
| Ponytail palm | *Beaucarnea recurvata* | A sculptural plant that tolerates dry soil |

Two herbs on the ASPCA's non-toxic list are useful in a kitchen with a cat: basil and rosemary. Note that catnip and mint are listed as toxic to cats, despite catnip's name.

## Common houseplants that are toxic to cats

Many of the most popular houseplants are on the ASPCA's toxic list. If you have a cat, keep these out of reach or out of the house:

- **Lilies** (*Lilium*) and **daylilies** (*Hemerocallis*). Life-threatening; see below.
- **Aroids**, which contain irritating calcium oxalate crystals: monstera, pothos, philodendron, peace lily, alocasia, dieffenbachia and Chinese evergreen. They cause mouth pain, drooling and vomiting.
- **Succulents**: aloe vera, jade plant and kalanchoe. String of pearls is not on the ASPCA list, but [NC State Extension](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/curio-rowleyanus/) rates it toxic to cats and dogs, with low severity.
- **Sago palm** (*Cycas revoluta*). Despite the name, it is not a true palm, and it is severely toxic.
- **Snake plant**, **dracaena**, **ficus** and **English ivy**.

Swapping a toxic plant for a safe one that looks similar is easier than keeping a cat away from it. An areca or parlour palm can replace a sago palm. Echeveria and haworthia can replace jade and aloe. A calathea gives the large patterned leaves people want from a monstera or alocasia.

## Why are lilies so dangerous to cats?

True lilies and daylilies are among the most dangerous plants for cats. The [US Food and Drug Administration](https://www.fda.gov/animal-veterinary/animal-health-literacy/lovely-lilies-and-curious-cats-dangerous-combination) warns that every part of the plant is toxic, including the pollen and even the water in the vase.

The FDA describes the timeline:

- **Within 0 to 12 hours:** early signs such as vomiting, loss of appetite and lethargy.
- **Within 12 to 24 hours:** signs of kidney damage.
- **Within 24 to 72 hours:** kidney failure without treatment.

Early treatment matters. The FDA notes that when treatment is delayed by 18 hours or more, the kidney failure is usually irreversible. If you think your cat has touched a lily, go to a vet straight away, even if it seems fine.

Peace lilies and calla lilies are not true lilies. They are aroids and cause mouth irritation rather than kidney failure, but they are still on the toxic list.

## What to do if your cat eats a plant

1. **Remove any plant material** from your cat's mouth and move the plant away.
2. **Identify the plant.** Check the label, or take a photo and a piece of the plant with you.
3. **Call your vet,** or the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center on (888) 426-4435 in the US. A fee may apply.
4. **Do not try to make your cat vomit** unless a vet tells you to.

Watch for vomiting, drooling, pawing at the mouth, loss of appetite or lethargy. If the plant was a lily, do not wait for symptoms.

## How to keep cats away from houseplants

- **Hang plants or use high shelves** a cat cannot jump onto. Trailing plants work well in hanging baskets.
- **Give your cat its own plant.** A pot of cat grass (wheat or oat grass) often draws attention away from your other plants.
- **Pick up fallen leaves and flowers** before your cat plays with them.
- **Check before you buy.** Search the plant's botanical name on the ASPCA list, not only its common name. Common names vary, and several unrelated plants are sold as "palm" or "lily".
