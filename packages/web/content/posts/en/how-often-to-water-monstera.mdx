How often to water monstera depends on the season more than anything else: about every 7 to 10 days in spring and summer, and every 2 to 3 weeks in winter, in a bright room. The real rule is to water once the top quarter to third of the soil has dried out, which is what [NC State Extension](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/monstera-deliciosa/) recommends. Check the soil before every watering rather than following the calendar.

## When to water a monstera

Push a finger or a wooden chopstick into the soil. Water when the top quarter to third of the pot is dry:

- **20 cm (8 in) pot:** dry about 5 cm (2 in) down.
- **30 cm (12 in) pot:** dry about 8 to 10 cm (3 to 4 in) down.

The [University of Maryland Extension](https://extension.umd.edu/resource/watering-indoor-plants) suggests a second check: lift the pot. A dry pot is much lighter than one that has just been watered. After a few weeks you can tell by weight alone.

When it is time, water slowly over the whole surface until water runs out of the drainage hole. Wait 15 to 20 minutes and empty the saucer or cachepot. A monstera should never stand in water.

## How often to water monstera, season by season

These are starting points for a mature monstera in a 20 to 30 cm (8 to 12 in) pot with a drainage hole, in a bright room. Adjust from there based on what the soil tells you.

| Season | Typical interval | What is happening |
|---|---|---|
| Spring | Every 7 to 10 days | Growth restarts as days lengthen |
| Summer | Every 5 to 10 days | Peak growth and warmth, fastest drying |
| Autumn | Every 10 to 14 days | Growth slows with shorter days |
| Winter | Every 2 to 3 weeks | Little growth, soil stays wet longer |

Winter is when most monsteras get overwatered. Days are short, the plant barely grows, and a pot watered on the summer schedule stays wet for weeks. [Lily](/en) lengthens each plant's watering interval as indoor daylight falls, so the schedule follows the season instead of staying fixed.

## What changes how often you water

- **Light.** The single biggest factor. A monstera close to a bright window drinks far more than one in a dim corner.
- **Temperature.** NC State gives 15 to 29°C (60 to 85°F) as the comfortable range. Warmer rooms dry pots faster.
- **Pot size and material.** Small pots dry faster than big ones. Terracotta dries faster than plastic or glazed ceramic.
- **Potting mix.** A chunky aroid mix with bark and perlite drains and dries faster than dense peat-based soil.
- **Plant size.** A large plant with many leaves in a snug pot can drink its pot dry within a few days in summer.
- **Humidity.** Dry, heated air in winter speeds up drying slightly. It does not cancel out the slower growth.

## Signs you are overwatering a monstera

- Lower leaves turning yellow, often several at once.
- Brown patches with a yellow halo in the middle of leaves.
- Soil that is still wet a week after watering.
- Soft, dark stems near the soil, or a sour smell from the pot.
- Fungus gnats around the pot.

If you see these, stop watering and let the top third of the pot dry out. If yellowing continues, check the roots. Our guide to [monstera problems](/en/blog/monstera-problems) walks through rescuing a plant with root rot.

## Signs you are underwatering a monstera

- Leaves drooping or curling inward.
- Crispy brown edges, starting on older leaves.
- Soil pulling away from the sides of the pot.
- A very light pot.

A thirsty monstera recovers quickly. Water thoroughly, and if the soil has shrunk away from the pot, water twice, 20 minutes apart, so the mix rewets. The leaves should lift within a day. For a side-by-side comparison of both sets of symptoms, see [overwatering vs underwatering](/en/blog/overwatering-vs-underwatering).

## What kind of water to use

Tap water is fine for monstera in most homes. UMD Extension recommends avoiding water from a water softener, which contains more dissolved salts. Room-temperature water is gentler on roots than cold water from the tap. If you see a white crust on the soil or on the pot, flush the pot with plenty of plain water every few months, as UMD recommends.

## Can you grow a monstera in water?

Yes, a monstera cutting can live in water for a long time, and many people root cuttings that way. The cutting needs at least one node, the bump on the stem where a leaf and an aerial root grow from. A leaf on its own, without a node, will not grow into a plant.

1. **Cut below a node.** Use clean shears and cut 2 to 3 cm (1 in) below the node, keeping one or two leaves on the cutting.
2. **Submerge the node.** Place the cutting in a glass or jar so that the node and any aerial root are under water. Roots grow from the node, so it must be submerged. Keep the leaves above the waterline.
3. **Give it bright, indirect light.** No direct sun on the glass, which warms the water and encourages algae.
4. **Change the water every 5 to 7 days,** or as soon as it turns cloudy. Ordinary tap water at room temperature is fine. You do not need distilled water.
5. **Wait.** Roots usually appear within 2 to 6 weeks, depending on warmth and light.

A monstera can stay in water for months or longer. It grows more slowly than one in soil and runs out of nutrients, so feed it with a diluted liquid fertiliser once roots are established.

To move it into soil, wait until the roots are 5 to 8 cm (2 to 3 in) long. Pot it in a small pot with chunky aroid mix and keep the soil slightly moist for the first two or three weeks while the water roots adapt. Then switch to the normal schedule above.
