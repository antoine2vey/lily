Areca palm care comes down to four things: bright light without hot direct sun, soil that stays lightly moist but never soggy, humid air, and a room that does not drop below about 16 °C (60°F). The areca (Dypsis lutescens, also sold as butterfly palm or golden cane palm) comes from moist forests in Madagascar, and almost every brown frond you see indoors traces back to dry air, dry roots or cold. It is also one of the few large indoor palms that is safe around cats and dogs.

## Areca palm care at a glance

| Need | What it wants |
|---|---|
| Light | Bright, indirect. Close to an east window or 1 to 2 m (3 to 6 ft) back from a south or west window |
| Water | When the top 2 to 3 cm (1 in) of soil is dry, until water drains out. Less in winter |
| Humidity | High. Aim for 50% or more, with a humidifier in heated rooms |
| Temperature | Normal room temperature, never below 16 °C (60°F) |
| Soil | Well-drained potting mix in a pot with a drainage hole |
| Repotting | Every 2 to 3 years, in spring |
| Toxicity | Non-toxic to cats, dogs and horses |

The light, soil and humidity guidance comes from the [NC State Extension plant page](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/dypsis-lutescens/), the 16 °C floor from the [Missouri Botanical Garden](https://www.missouribotanicalgarden.org/PlantFinder/PlantFinderDetails.aspx?taxonid=291457), and the toxicity entry from the [ASPCA plant list](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/animal-poison-control/toxic-and-non-toxic-plants/areca-palm).

## How much light does an areca palm need?

A lot of light, but filtered. NC State recommends a bright spot with indirect light, and the Missouri Botanical Garden notes that full direct sun indoors can scorch the fronds. In practice that means right beside an east window, or set back from a south or west window, or behind a sheer curtain.

Signs the light is wrong:

- **Too little:** new fronds come in thin and pale, stems lean hard toward the window, and the soil stays wet for more than a week after watering.
- **Too much:** yellow-white bleached patches or dry brown scorch on the fronds facing the glass.

Turn the pot a quarter turn every couple of weeks so the clump grows evenly.

## How often should you water an areca palm?

Water when the top 2 to 3 cm (about 1 in) of soil feels dry, then water slowly until it runs out of the drainage hole. Empty the saucer after 15 minutes. NC State's advice is to keep the soil moist but not soggy during the growing season, and the Missouri Botanical Garden describes it as wanting consistent moisture.

In a bright, heated room that often works out to about once a week in summer and every 10 to 14 days in winter. Treat those numbers as a starting point. A large pot, a cool room or a dim corner all slow drying, so check with a finger before you water rather than following the calendar.

The two mistakes look different. Letting the pot dry out completely gives crisp brown tips across many fronds at once. Keeping it wet gives yellowing lower fronds, a sour smell from the soil and stems that go soft at the base. ## How do you raise humidity for an areca palm?

The areca likes humid air, and the air in a heated home in winter is often much drier than that. NC State suggests a humidifier if the air is dry. A cool-mist humidifier placed near the plant is the only method that raises humidity in a meaningful and lasting way.

Misting is often recommended for palms, but it raises the humidity around the leaves for only a few minutes and does nothing for the brown tips. Grouping plants together and keeping the palm away from radiators and heating vents help a little. Our article on [how to increase humidity for houseplants](/en/blog/how-to-increase-humidity-for-houseplants) compares the options.

## Why are my areca palm tips turning brown?

Brown tips are the most common complaint. In rough order of likelihood:

1. **Dry air.** The tips of many fronds brown at the same time, mostly in winter when the heating is on.
2. **Soil drying out completely between waterings.** Tips brown and the lowest fronds yellow and dry out.
3. **Salt buildup from fertiliser or hard tap water.** A white crust forms on the soil surface or the pot rim. Flush the pot by running water through it for a minute every few months.
4. **Cold or draughts.** Brown patches appear on fronds near a door or a window that is opened in winter.

Cut off brown tips with clean scissors, following the shape of the leaflet and leaving a thin line of brown so you do not cut into green tissue. A frond that is entirely brown can be cut at its base. Old lower fronds yellowing one at a time is normal ageing. Our page on [areca palm problems](/en/blog/areca-palm-problems) covers yellow fronds, spots and root rot.

## What soil and pot does an areca palm need?

A standard well-drained potting mix works. NC State only asks for good drainage. If your mix holds water for a long time, add about a quarter perlite or fine bark. Use a pot with a drainage hole, and skip the layer of gravel at the bottom: it raises the waterlogged zone instead of draining it. See our guide to the [best soil for houseplants](/en/blog/best-soil-for-houseplants).

Arecas grow as a clump of stems from the base, and they do not like having their roots disturbed. NC State suggests repotting every 2 or 3 years. Go up one size, around 5 cm (2 in) wider, in spring, and keep the palm at the same depth it was growing before.

## Can you propagate an areca palm?

Not from cuttings. A palm stem has a single growing point at the top, so a cut stem will not root. The practical way to get more plants is division: in spring, slide the clump out of its pot and pull or cut apart sections that each have several stems and their own roots. Pot each one in fresh mix and keep it out of direct sun for a few weeks while it recovers. Expect some brown fronds afterwards.

Nursery arecas are often several seedlings planted together in one pot, which makes division easier than it sounds.

## Should you fertilise an areca palm?

Lightly. Use a balanced liquid houseplant fertiliser during spring and summer, following the label rate, and stop in autumn and winter when growth slows. Overfeeding is a common cause of brown tips through salt buildup.

## What pests attack areca palms?

NC State and the Missouri Botanical Garden list scale insects, whiteflies and spider mites. Spider mites are the most likely indoors, because they multiply in warm, dry air. Look under the fronds for fine webbing and pale speckling on the leaflets. Rinsing the whole plant in the shower and raising humidity both help. Our guide to [spider mite treatment](/en/blog/spider-mites-treatment) has the full routine.

Scale looks like small brown bumps stuck to the stems and the undersides of the leaflets. Scrape them off or wipe them with a cotton swab dipped in rubbing alcohol, and check again every week for a month.

## Is an areca palm safe for cats and dogs?

Yes. The ASPCA lists Dypsis lutescens as non-toxic to dogs, cats and horses. Be careful with the name "palm", though. The sago palm (Cycas revoluta), often sold next to arecas, is a cycad, not a palm, and it is highly toxic to pets.
