How often to water a peace lily depends on the soil, not the calendar: water when the top 2 to 3 cm (1 in) has dried. In a warm, bright room that is often about once a week in summer, and every 10 to 14 days in winter. [Clemson Extension](https://hgic.clemson.edu/factsheet/peace-lily/) sums up the target as soil kept moist but not soggy and allowed to dry out slightly between waterings.

## How to tell when a peace lily needs water

Check before you water, every time:

- **Finger test.** Push a finger into the soil. If the top 2 to 3 cm (1 in) is dry, water. If it still feels damp, wait and check again in two or three days.
- **Pot weight.** Lift the pot just after watering, then again a few days later. A dry pot is noticeably lighter, and after a few rounds you can tell by weight alone.
- **Slight droop.** A peace lily starts to droop as soon as it runs short of water, which makes it one of the easiest plants to read. A slight droop with dry soil means water now. Do not wait for it to collapse every time, though: Clemson notes that soil allowed to dry out excessively makes the plant wilt and the leaves and leaf edges turn yellow.

A droop with **wet** soil means something else entirely, usually roots that are rotting. Do not water it again. Our guide to [peace lily problems](/en/blog/peace-lily-problems) explains how to tell the two apart.

## How often to water a peace lily in each season

These intervals are starting points for a plant in a 15 to 20 cm (6 to 8 in) pot with drainage, in a medium to bright room. Use them to know when to start checking, then water when the soil says so.

| Season | Typical interval | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Spring | Every 7 to 10 days | Growth picks up as days lengthen |
| Summer | Every 5 to 7 days | Warmth and long days dry the soil fastest |
| Autumn | Every 7 to 10 days | Shorter days, slower growth |
| Winter | Every 10 to 14 days | Little growth; the soil stays wet much longer |

Winter is when most peace lilies get overwatered. The plant barely grows, and the soil can stay damp for two weeks in a cool room. Keeping the summer rhythm is the usual route to yellow leaves and root rot.

## What changes the watering interval

- **Light.** A peace lily in bright, indirect light uses far more water than one in a dim corner. In low light, the interval can stretch to two weeks or more even in summer.
- **Temperature.** Clemson gives 20 to 29°C (68 to 85°F) by day as the comfortable range. Warmer rooms and radiators dry the soil faster. A cool room slows everything down.
- **Pot size and material.** A plant whose roots fill the pot dries out in a few days. Clemson says peace lilies like to be slightly pot-bound, but repot when roots grow through the drainage holes or circle on the soil surface. A pot much bigger than the root ball holds water the roots cannot reach, and terracotta dries faster than plastic. Our guide to [choosing a pot](/en/blog/how-to-choose-a-pot-for-your-plant) covers this.
- **Soil.** Clemson notes peace lilies grow in almost any well-drained potting mix. A dense, old mix that stays wet needs longer gaps.
- **Humidity.** Dry indoor air in a heated home speeds up drying. [NC State Extension](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/spathiphyllum/) lists low humidity as one cause of brown leaf tips.

## How to water a peace lily

1. **Water thoroughly.** Pour slowly over the whole soil surface until water runs from the drainage hole. Small sips keep the top wet and the bottom of the root ball dry.
2. **Empty the saucer.** Clemson warns that a plant left sitting in a saucer of water will develop root rot. Tip out what drains through after 15 minutes or so. A cachepot without a hole counts as a saucer.
3. **Use room-temperature water.** Clemson recommends it. Cold water straight from the tap in winter can chill the roots.
4. **Or water from below.** NC State says bottom watering works very well for peace lilies. Stand the pot in a few centimetres of water for 20 to 30 minutes, until the top of the soil feels moist, then let it drain.
5. **Flush now and then.** NC State notes that over-fertilising and salt build-up can burn leaf tips and roots. Every few months, water slowly from the top and let plenty run through to rinse out salts, especially if you bottom-water.

## Signs of overwatering

- Several leaves yellowing at once, starting low on the plant
- Drooping while the soil is still wet
- Dark, soft stems at soil level, or a sour smell from the pot
- Soil that is still damp a week or more after watering
- Fungus gnats around the pot

Clemson names root and stem diseases as the most common peace lily problem, usually from overwatering. If the soil stays wet and the plant droops, take it out of the pot and look at the roots. Our [root rot recovery guide](/en/blog/root-rot-recovery) explains what to do.

## Signs of underwatering

- Drooping with dry soil and a light pot, recovering within hours of watering
- Leaf edges and older leaves turning yellow after repeated dry spells
- Brown, crisp leaf tips (NC State also links these to too much sun and low humidity)
- Soil pulling away from the side of the pot

If the root ball has dried out so far that water runs straight down the gap, bottom-water it for half an hour so the whole root ball soaks again. If your peace lily wilts every few days, it may have outgrown its pot. For a side-by-side checklist, see [overwatering vs underwatering](/en/blog/overwatering-vs-underwatering).

## Quick answers

**Should I water a peace lily once a week?** Only if the soil is dry by then. Check first. Once a week fits many plants in summer and is too often for most in winter.

**Should I mist a peace lily?** Misting wets the leaves, not the roots, so it is no substitute for watering the soil.

**What if I go away for two weeks?** Water thoroughly just before you leave and move the plant back from the window. Our guide to [watering plants while on vacation](/en/blog/how-to-water-plants-while-on-vacation) has longer options.

[Lily](/en) schedules watering for each plant and adjusts the indoor interval to the season's daylight, so a peace lily's reminders stretch out in winter.
