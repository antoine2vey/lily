Anthurium care comes down to bright light with no direct midday sun, watering once the top 2.5 cm (1 in) of soil is dry, and a warm room between 18 and 27°C (65 and 80°F). Give it an airy, fast-draining mix rather than dense potting compost, because its roots rot quickly in soggy soil. With enough light, a healthy anthurium keeps producing its red, pink or white "flowers" for most of the year.

## Anthurium care at a glance

| Need | What to give it |
|---|---|
| Light | Bright, indirect light. A metre (3 ft) from a south or west window, or right beside an east one |
| Water | When the top 2.5 cm (1 in) is dry, then water until it drains |
| Humidity | 60 to 80% is ideal. A humidifier helps in heated rooms in winter |
| Temperature | 18 to 27°C (65 to 80°F). No cold draughts, no sudden swings |
| Soil | 1 part orchid bark, 1 part peat or coir, 1 part perlite, in a pot with a drainage hole |
| Feeding | Diluted liquid fertiliser in spring and summer, none in winter |
| Toxicity | Toxic to cats and dogs (insoluble calcium oxalate crystals) |

The light, water, humidity and temperature figures come from [NC State Extension's profile of Anthurium andraeanum](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/anthurium-andraeanum/). The plant is native to the rainforests of Colombia and Ecuador, where many grow as epiphytes, rooted in moss and bark on tree trunks rather than in the ground. That explains most of what it wants in a pot: light filtered through leaves, warm humid air, and roots that dry out a little between rains.

## How much light does an anthurium need?

More than most people give it. An anthurium survives in a dim corner, but it stops flowering there. NC State lists low light as one of the main reasons an anthurium does not bloom.

Good spots are right beside an east-facing window, a metre or so back from a south- or west-facing one, or behind a sheer curtain. Direct sun through glass at midday scorches the leaves, leaving dry brown or bleached patches on the side facing the window.

Signs it needs more light:

- No new flowers for several months even though the plant looks healthy.
- Long, thin leaf stalks that lean hard toward the window.
- The soil takes more than ten days to dry after watering.

In a dark flat, a full-spectrum LED grow light 30 cm (12 in) above the plant for 12 hours a day is a reasonable substitute. Our [houseplant light guide](/en/blog/houseplant-light-guide) explains how to judge a spot by its window direction.

## How often should you water an anthurium?

Water when the top 2.5 cm (1 in) of soil feels dry, which is NC State's guideline. Push a finger in to check. When it is dry to that depth, water slowly until water runs out of the drainage hole, then empty the saucer after 15 minutes.

In a bright, warm room that often means about once a week in summer and every 10 to 14 days in winter. Treat those numbers as a starting point, not a schedule. A bark-based mix dries faster than compost, a large pot slower, and a cool room much slower.

Overwatering is the most common way anthuriums die. NC State names overwatering and poor drainage as causes of both yellow leaves and root rot. Soil that is still wet a week after watering, yellowing lower leaves and dark, soft stem bases are the warning signs. Underwatering shows as drooping leaves that perk up within a day of watering, and as brown, crisp leaf edges. Our article on [how often to water an anthurium](/en/blog/how-often-to-water-anthurium) goes through the season and pot factors in detail.

[Lily](/en) schedules watering per plant and shortens or stretches the interval with the season's daylight, which is when a fixed weekly routine starts to drown an anthurium.

## Humidity and temperature

NC State gives 60 to 80% as the ideal humidity. Most homes run drier than that, especially with the heating on, and the plant usually copes. Very dry air shows up as brown, papery leaf tips and new leaves that crinkle as they unfurl.

The only thing that raises room humidity by a useful amount is a humidifier. A tray of wet pebbles under the pot helps a little right around the plant. Skip misting: the water evaporates within minutes, and NC State notes that wet foliage and poor air circulation encourage leaf spots.

Keep it between 18 and 27°C (65 and 80°F). Anthuriums do not tolerate cold draughts or sudden temperature changes, so keep it away from a door that opens in winter, a cold window pane, radiators and the direct blast of air conditioning.

## Best soil for an anthurium

Because it grows on tree bark in the wild, an anthurium wants a mix with lots of air in it. Dense, peat-only compost stays wet in the centre of the pot and suffocates the roots. A good homemade mix is equal parts:

- orchid bark
- peat moss or coconut coir
- perlite

A bagged "aroid" or orchid-and-houseplant mix is close to this. Always use a pot with a drainage hole. Our guide to the [best soil for houseplants](/en/blog/best-soil-for-houseplants) explains why chunky mixes work for epiphytes.

## How to get an anthurium to bloom

The colourful part is a spathe, a modified leaf. The true flowers are the tiny bumps along the spike in the middle, the spadix. A well-grown anthurium produces new spathes through most of the year. If yours has stopped, check these in order:

1. **Light.** The most common reason. Move it closer to a bright window.
2. **Feeding.** NC State also names nutrient shortage as a cause. Feed with a liquid fertiliser during spring and summer. NC State suggests feeding weekly in the growing season; at that frequency, dilute it to a quarter or half of the label strength so salts do not build up. A fertiliser with a little more phosphorus than nitrogen is often sold for flowering plants.
3. **Age of the plant.** A small offset or a freshly divided plant needs time to build up before it flowers again.

Cut each spathe off at the base of its stalk once it fades or turns green. Stop feeding in late autumn and winter, when the plant grows slowly.

## When to repot an anthurium

NC State recommends repotting every two to three years, or when the plant is root-bound, ideally in spring. Signs it is time: roots growing out of the drainage hole, roots circling the surface, or a pot that dries out in two or three days.

1. Water the day before so the root ball slides out in one piece.
2. Shake off the old mix and cut away any brown, soft roots.
3. Move it into a pot only 2 to 5 cm (1 to 2 in) wider. An oversized pot holds water the roots cannot use.
4. Set it at the same depth, fill with fresh bark mix and water until it drains.

Older anthuriums often grow a short stem with aerial roots above the soil. That is normal. You can bury it a little deeper at repotting time so the aerial roots reach the mix. See our [spring repotting guide](/en/blog/spring-repotting-guide) for the general method.

## How to propagate an anthurium

NC State lists division and stem cuttings. Division is the easiest. When you repot a plant with several crowns, pull or cut it apart so each piece keeps its own leaves and roots, then pot each one in fresh mix. A stem cutting needs at least one node with an aerial root. Pot it in damp sphagnum moss or bark mix, keep it warm and bright, and it usually roots within a few weeks.

## Common anthurium problems

- **Yellow leaves:** usually soil kept too wet or a pot that does not drain. One old leaf yellowing now and then is normal.
- **Brown leaf tips:** dry air, soil left dry too long, or fertiliser salts.
- **Brown or black spots:** leaf spot disease, favoured by wet leaves and still air, or sun scorch on the leaves facing the window.
- **No flowers:** too little light or too little food.
- **Pests:** NC State lists aphids, spider mites, mealybugs and scale. Check the undersides of leaves and where the leaf stalks meet the stem.

Each of these has its own diagnosis in our article on [anthurium problems](/en/blog/anthurium-problems).

## Is anthurium toxic to cats and dogs?

Yes. The [ASPCA](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/aspca-poison-control/toxic-and-non-toxic-plants/flamingo-flower) lists anthurium as toxic to dogs, cats and horses. Like monstera and peace lily, it contains insoluble calcium oxalate crystals. Chewing a leaf causes pain and swelling in the mouth, drooling, vomiting and difficulty swallowing. NC State rates the severity as low, but keep it out of reach of pets that chew plants. The sap can irritate skin, so wear gloves when you divide or prune it.
