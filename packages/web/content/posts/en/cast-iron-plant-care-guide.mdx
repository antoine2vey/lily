Cast iron plant care comes down to shade and restraint: keep it out of direct sun, water when the top 5 cm (2 in) of soil is dry, and water much less from late autumn to early spring. NC State rates its growth as slow, often only a few new leaves a year, and it reaches 30 to 90 cm (1 to 3 ft). It is non-toxic to cats and dogs, and it has no serious pest problems.

## Cast iron plant care at a glance

| Need | What to give it |
|---|---|
| Light | Shade to bright indirect light, such as a north window or 2 to 3 m (6 to 10 ft) back from a sunny one. No direct sun. |
| Water | When the top 5 cm (2 in) is dry, roughly every 1 to 2 weeks in summer. Every 3 to 4 weeks in winter. |
| Humidity | Ordinary room air. No humidifier or pebble tray needed. |
| Temperature | Any normal room temperature. Hardy outdoors down to about -15 °C (5 °F). |
| Soil | Any well-drained potting mix, in a pot with a drainage hole. |
| Pets | Non-toxic to cats, dogs and horses. |
| Repotting | Every 2 to 4 years, in spring, 2 to 5 cm (1 to 2 in) wider. |

The plant is *Aspidistra elatior*, native to Japan. [NC State Extension](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/aspidistra-elatior/) describes it as preferring partial to full shade, intolerant of direct sun, and drought tolerant. The common name comes from its ability to withstand neglect. The same page tags it non-toxic for cats, dogs and horses, which matches the [ASPCA entry](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/animal-poison-control/toxic-and-non-toxic-plants/cast-iron-plant).

## How much light does a cast iron plant need?

Less than almost any other houseplant. It grows in hallways, on bookshelves away from the window, and in north-facing rooms where most plants stretch and fade. A spot where you can read a book in daytime without a lamp is enough.

What it does not tolerate is direct sun. A south or west window that puts sun on the leaves for a few hours bleaches them to a yellowish green, then burns pale brown patches into the blades. If you see that, move it back. The burnt patches will not turn green again, so cut those leaves off at the base once new ones come up.

Variegated cultivars, with white or cream stripes or spots, probably need a little more light than the plain green form, since the pale parts of the leaf make less food. Keep them in bright indirect light, still out of direct sun.

More light makes it grow a little faster, not differently. In deep shade it simply produces fewer leaves.

## How often to water a cast iron plant

Water when the top 5 cm (2 in) of soil is dry. Push a finger in to the second knuckle. If it feels damp, wait. If it is dry, water slowly until water runs out of the drainage hole, then empty the saucer after 15 minutes.

In a warm, bright room in summer, that usually lands at every 1 to 2 weeks. In winter, when the plant barely grows, every 3 to 4 weeks is common. NC State's advice is to water regularly from spring to autumn and sparingly in winter. The pot, the mix and the room change the interval, so check the soil rather than the calendar. The [cast iron plant watering guide](/en/blog/how-often-to-water-cast-iron-plant) goes through the seasons in more detail.

The plant forgives a missed watering far better than too many. Its thick rhizomes store water. Soil that stays wet for weeks rots those rhizomes and the roots, and that is how most cast iron plants die indoors.

[Clemson Extension](https://hgic.clemson.edu/factsheet/indoor-plants-watering/) recommends room-temperature water, around 17 to 22 °C (62 to 72 °F), and advises against softened water because of its sodium.

## What soil and pot does it need?

Any good houseplant potting mix works, as long as it drains. NC State notes that it tolerates poor and dry soil but does best in rich, well-drained soil. If your mix stays wet for more than a week after watering, add a few handfuls of perlite or fine bark.

The pot matters more than the mix. It needs a drainage hole. A decorative pot without one is fine as an outer cover, as long as you tip out the water that collects in it. Terracotta dries faster than plastic, which suits a heavy-handed waterer. The [houseplant soil guide](/en/blog/best-soil-for-houseplants) covers mixes in more depth.

## When to repot a cast iron plant

Every 2 to 4 years, in spring. It grows slowly and does not mind being slightly crowded. Repot when roots come out of the drainage hole, when the rhizomes press against the pot wall, or when water runs straight through without wetting the soil.

Move up only 2 to 5 cm (1 to 2 in) in diameter. A much larger pot holds a lot of wet soil the roots cannot reach, which is the setup for rot. Set the rhizomes at the same depth as before, water once, and then let the top of the soil dry as usual. The [repotting guide](/en/blog/spring-repotting-guide) walks through the steps.

## How to propagate a cast iron plant

By division. The plant spreads by rhizomes, horizontal stems just under the soil surface, and each leaf grows from one. When you repot in spring:

1. Slide the plant out and shake or wash off enough soil to see the rhizomes.
2. Cut through a rhizome with a clean knife so each piece has at least 3 or 4 leaves and its own roots.
3. Pot each piece in fresh mix at the same depth.
4. Water once and keep the soil barely moist until new leaves appear, which can take a few months.

Small divisions with one or two leaves can survive, but they take years to fill a pot. Larger divisions look like a plant from the start.

## Feeding and cleaning

Feed little. A balanced liquid fertiliser at half strength once a month from spring to late summer is plenty. Too much fertiliser builds up salts in the pot, which brown the leaf tips. Skip feeding from autumn to the end of winter.

The broad leaves collect dust, which cuts the little light they get. Wipe them with a damp cloth every month or two, supporting the leaf from below. Skip leaf shine products.

## Cast iron plant in autumn and winter

Indoors, almost nothing changes in autumn except watering. As days shorten and the heating comes on, growth stops. Stretch the interval between waterings and let the top 5 cm dry fully before you water again. Stop feeding.

Keep it away from radiators and heating vents, which dry the soil unevenly and can brown leaf edges. It tolerates cool rooms well. An unheated hallway or a cool bedroom suits it in winter, as long as it does not freeze.

If you keep a cast iron plant on a balcony or patio in summer, bring it in before the first frost. NC State lists it as hardy in USDA zones 7a to 11b, so in the ground it survives winter lows of about -15 °C (5 °F) when sheltered from winter wind. A plant in a pot is less protected, because the roots freeze before soil in the ground does.

## Why are my cast iron plant leaves turning yellow or brown?

A few points cover most cases. The [cast iron plant problems](/en/blog/cast-iron-plant-problems) page goes into each one.

- **One old leaf yellowing now and then** is normal. Leaves are long-lived, but old ones eventually die. Cut it at the base.
- **Several leaves yellowing, soil wet, smell from the pot** points to overwatering and root rot. Unpot it and check the rhizomes. Firm and pale is healthy. Soft, dark and smelly is rot. Cut out the rotten parts and repot in fresh, dry mix. The [root rot guide](/en/blog/root-rot-recovery) has the full method.
- **Bleached or brown patches in the middle of the leaf** mean direct sun.
- **Brown tips and edges** come from dry, salty or very cold conditions: soil dried out completely for weeks, fertiliser salts building up, or a cold draught. Flush the pot with plenty of water once to remove salts, then fix the cause.
- **Split leaves** are usually physical damage from being brushed against. Cut the leaf off if it bothers you.

NC State reports no serious pest problems. Indoors, check the leaf undersides now and then for spider mites or scale, especially on plants kept in warm, dry rooms.

## Quick answers

**Will a cast iron plant grow without a window?** Not for long. It needs some daylight or a grow light. Under a lamp switched on 10 to 12 hours a day, it can grow in a windowless room.

**Why is my cast iron plant not growing?** It may be. A handful of new leaves a year is normal for this slow grower, and they appear in spring and summer. Deep shade and a cold room slow it further.

**Does it flower?** Rarely, and you may not notice when it does. NC State describes small, fleshy, cup-shaped flowers in early summer that open close to the ground, under the leaves, and notes that the plant needs more light to bloom.
