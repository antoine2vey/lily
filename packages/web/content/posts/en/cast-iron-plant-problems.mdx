Cast iron plant problems are rare, and most of them trace back to two causes: soil kept too wet or leaves exposed to direct sun. Water an aspidistra only when the top 5 to 7 cm (2 to 3 in) of soil is dry, keep it out of direct sunlight, and check the undersides of the leaves for spider mites and scale every few weeks. It grows slowly, so damage takes months to grow out.

## Why are my cast iron plant leaves turning brown at the tips?

**1. Soil kept too wet.** Surprisingly, this is the most common cause. The [RHS](https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/aspidistra/how-to-grow-aspidistras) warns that soggy compost rots the roots, and damaged roots cannot supply the leaf tips with water, so the tips brown. If the soil is still damp a week after watering, or the pot sits in water, start here. [Clemson Cooperative Extension](https://hgic.clemson.edu/cast-iron-plant/) advises watering only when the soil is dry 5 to 7 cm (2 to 3 in) down and never leaving water in the saucer.

**2. Soil left bone dry for too long.** Aspidistras tolerate drought better than most houseplants, but a pot that dries out completely again and again browns the tips. The soil pulls away from the pot edge and the whole pot feels light.

**3. Salts from fertiliser or hard water.** A white crust on the soil surface or the rim of the pot points to salt build-up. The plant needs little feeding; the RHS suggests a monthly feed from spring to late summer only. Flush the pot with plain water every few months by watering heavily until it drains freely for a minute.

**4. Dry air near a heater.** Hot, dry air blowing on the leaves in winter can brown the edges. Move it away from radiators and vents.

**The fix.** Brown tips do not turn green again. You can trim them with clean scissors, following the natural pointed shape of the leaf and leaving a thin line of brown so you do not cut into healthy tissue. Then correct the cause, or the new tips will brown too.

## Why are my cast iron plant leaves turning yellow?

**1. Overwatering and root rot.** The most likely cause when several leaves yellow at once, especially if they also go soft at the base. Check the roots: healthy ones are firm and pale, rotten ones are dark and mushy. Let the soil dry well before watering again. If the roots are badly rotted, repot in fresh mix after cutting away the rotten parts. Our [root rot recovery](/en/blog/root-rot-recovery) guide covers the steps.

**2. Old leaves dying back.** Each leaf lives for years, but the oldest ones eventually yellow and die. One leaf at a time, on a plant that is otherwise producing new shoots, is normal. Cut it off at soil level.

**3. Spider mites.** Fine yellow or pale speckling, rather than whole yellow leaves, points to mites. See the pests section below.

**4. Too much sun.** Leaves in strong light fade to a washed-out yellow-green before they scorch.

## Why does my cast iron plant have brown or bleached patches?

**1. Sun scorch.** [NC State Extension](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/aspidistra-elatior/) describes the plant as intolerant of direct sun, and Clemson notes that sun causes leaf scorch. Scorched areas are dry, pale or brown patches in the middle of the leaf, on the side facing the window. Move the plant out of direct sun. A spot 2 m (6 ft) or more from a bright window, or a north-facing room, suits it.

**2. Cold damage.** The RHS gives a range of 5 to 20°C (40 to 70°F), so it handles cool rooms well, but leaves pressed against a frosty window can develop dark, papery patches.

**3. Leaf spot disease.** Round brown spots, sometimes with a yellow halo, are usually a fungal leaf spot. They are encouraged by water left sitting on the leaves. Remove the worst leaves, water the soil rather than the foliage, and give the plant some space for air to move.

## Why is my cast iron plant drooping or falling over?

**1. Root rot.** If leaves flop and the soil is wet, check the roots as above.

**2. Thirst.** Leaves that hang limp in dry soil and stand back up within a day of watering just needed water.

**3. Weak, stretched leaves.** In very dark corners, new leaves can be long and floppy. Aspidistra tolerates deep shade, but it still needs some daylight.

## Why has my variegated aspidistra turned plain green?

The RHS notes that variegated aspidistras revert to plain green in poor light. Move it to brighter, indirect light, with no direct sun. Cut any fully green leaves off at soil level so they do not take over, since green leaves grow more vigorously than variegated ones.

## Why is my cast iron plant not growing?

Probably because it is a cast iron plant. NC State describes it as slow growing, and in a shady room it may produce only a few new leaves a year. Check for problems only if no new shoots appear in spring and summer for a year or more. If so, it may need repotting. The RHS suggests repotting in spring when roots start to grow through the drainage holes. More light (still indirect) and a light monthly feed in spring and summer also help.

## What pests attack cast iron plants?

The RHS lists three. Clemson recommends checking weekly for mites and scale.

**Spider mites.** The most common pest. Look for fine, pale speckling on the leaves; the RHS describes leaves that look speckled or bleached. Under a magnifying glass you can see tiny moving dots on the undersides, and sometimes fine webbing. They spread fast in warm, dry indoor air.

To treat them:

1. Wipe both sides of every leaf with a damp cloth, or rinse the plant in the shower.
2. Repeat every 4 to 5 days for at least three weeks, since eggs keep hatching.
3. For heavy infestations, use insecticidal soap, following the label, and test it on one leaf first.

Our article on [spider mites treatment](/en/blog/spider-mites-treatment) goes into more detail.

**Scale insects.** Small, brown, oval bumps on the leaves and stems, often along the midrib on the underside. They leave sticky honeydew on the leaves below. Scrape them off with a fingernail or a soft toothbrush, or wipe them off with a cotton bud dipped in rubbing alcohol. Check again every week for a month.

**Mealybugs.** White, cottony clusters at the base of the leaf stalks and in the folds of young leaves. Remove them with a cotton bud dipped in rubbing alcohol.

## How to prevent cast iron plant problems

- Water only when the top 5 to 7 cm (2 to 3 in) of soil is dry, and a little less in winter.
- Keep it out of direct sun.
- Use a pot with a drainage hole and empty the saucer after watering.
- Dust the leaves with a damp cloth every month or two, which also lets you spot pests early.
- Feed lightly, only in spring and summer.
