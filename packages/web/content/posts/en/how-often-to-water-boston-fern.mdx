How often to water a Boston fern comes down to one rule: water as soon as the surface of the soil starts to feel dry, which in a warm, bright room usually means every 3 to 5 days in summer and every 7 to 10 days in winter. [NC State Extension](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/nephrolepis-exaltata/) says the soil of Nephrolepis exaltata should never be allowed to dry out, and that you should water less in winter, when the plant is dormant and soggy soil leads to root rot. The goal is soil that stays evenly moist, like a wrung-out sponge, never dry and never waterlogged.

## How do you know when a Boston fern needs water?

Boston ferns give you less warning than succulents, so check often. Every two or three days in summer is a good habit.

- **Touch the surface.** Press a finger on the soil. When the top 1 to 2 cm (about 1/2 in) feels dry, water. Do not wait until the soil is dry deeper down.
- **Lift the pot.** A Boston fern in a light peat-based mix gets noticeably lighter as it dries. A light pot means it needs water now.
- **Watch the fronds.** Fronds that lose their shine, look greyish or start to droop mean the fern is already short of water. Leaflets that dry and drop after the pot has dried out once or twice are the next stage.

The [NYBG plant information service](https://libanswers.nybg.org/faq/222598) gives the same target as NC State: keep the root ball moist. A fern can lose many fronds after a single complete drying, so in summer it is better to check a day early than a day late.

## How often to water a Boston fern by season

| Season | Starting interval | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Spring (March to May) | Every 4 to 7 days | Growth restarts; check twice a week |
| Summer (June to August) | Every 3 to 5 days | A fern in a hanging basket or a warm room may need water every 2 days |
| Autumn (September to November) | Every 5 to 8 days | Stretch the gap as days shorten |
| Winter (December to February) | Every 7 to 10 days | Water less, but do not let the soil dry out; heating can dry pots quickly |

These intervals are rules of thumb for an indoor fern in a 20 cm (8 in) plastic pot with a drainage hole, in bright indirect light at 16 to 21 °C (60 to 70°F), the range NC State recommends. Use them to know when to start checking, then let the soil decide.

Winter is the tricky season. NC State notes that Boston ferns are dormant in winter and overwatering then causes root rot, but a fern next to a radiator can still dry out fast. Check the soil, not the calendar: in a cool room, the pot may stay moist for ten days; above a heater, it may need water every five. Our guide to [houseplant care in winter](/en/blog/houseplant-care-in-winter) covers the other seasonal changes.

## How do you water a Boston fern?

1. Water slowly over the whole surface until water runs out of the drainage hole.
2. Let it drain for 10 to 15 minutes, then empty the saucer or cachepot. A fern standing in water for days will rot.
3. If the root ball has dried out and shrunk from the edge of the pot, water runs straight down the sides without wetting it. Stand the pot in a bowl of water for 20 to 30 minutes instead, then let it drain.

Ordinary tap water at room temperature is fine. If your water is very hard and a white crust forms on the soil, flush the pot with plenty of water from time to time or use rainwater.

Do not add a layer of gravel at the bottom of the pot for drainage. It moves the waterlogged zone up into the roots instead of draining it away. A pot with a hole and a peat- or coir-based mix is enough.

## What changes how often a Boston fern needs water?

- **Light.** More light means faster drying. A fern in bright indirect light near a window uses water much faster than one at the back of a room. NYBG notes that too little light makes the fern shed leaflets continually, so a dim spot is not a fix for frequent watering.
- **Temperature and heating.** Warm air dries pots quickly. Radiators and heating vents can halve the time between waterings in winter.
- **Pot.** Terracotta and hanging baskets dry out faster than plastic or glazed ceramic. A fern that has filled its pot with roots also dries fast; NYBG recommends repotting in spring into a pot one size larger.
- **Humidity.** In dry air the fronds lose more water. Boston ferns need high humidity, and NC State suggests kitchens, bathrooms or a pebble tray under the pot. A humidifier works best. Misting only wets the fronds for a few minutes and does not water the plant. Our guide on [how to increase humidity for houseplants](/en/blog/how-to-increase-humidity-for-houseplants) compares the options.

## Signs of underwatering a Boston fern

- Fronds look dull or grey-green, then droop.
- Leaflets turn brown and crispy, often across the whole plant at once.
- Small leaflets drop onto the floor when you touch the plant.
- The pot is light and the soil has pulled away from the edge.

Soak the pot as described above. Cut off fronds that have turned fully brown, because they will not recover. New fronds grow from the centre within a few weeks if the fern is kept evenly moist.

## Signs of overwatering a Boston fern

- Fronds yellow and wilt even though the soil is wet.
- The soil stays soggy for days and smells sour.
- The centre of the plant (the crown) feels soft.
- Roots are brown, soft and come apart when pulled.

Let the surface dry slightly before the next watering, empty the saucer, and check that the drainage hole is not blocked. If the roots are rotting, our guide to [root rot recovery](/en/blog/root-rot-recovery) explains how to trim and repot. For other symptoms, see [Boston fern problems](/en/blog/boston-fern-problems).

Because a Boston fern needs water so often, it is easy to lose track. [Lily](/en) can set a watering schedule for each plant and space it out as the days get shorter, so the fern gets more water in summer and less in winter.

## Quick answers

**Can you water a Boston fern from the bottom?** Yes. Standing the pot in water for 20 to 30 minutes wets the whole root ball and is the easiest way to rewet a fern that has dried out. Drain it afterwards.
