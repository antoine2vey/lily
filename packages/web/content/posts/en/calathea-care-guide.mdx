Calathea care comes down to steady conditions: bright but indirect light, a mix that stays evenly moist without turning soggy, temperatures of 18 to 24°C (65 to 75°F) and humidity around 60%. Water with rain, distilled or filtered water if your tap water browns the leaves. Calatheas are safe for cats and dogs, but they show every lapse in humidity or watering as brown edges or curled leaves.

## Calathea care at a glance

| Need | What it wants |
|---|---|
| Light | Bright, indirect light or partial shade. No direct midday sun |
| Water | Keep the mix evenly moist, never wet. Water less in winter |
| Humidity | About 60%. Dry air rolls and browns the leaves |
| Temperature | 18 to 24°C (65 to 75°F), away from cold drafts |
| Soil | Moist, well-drained mix rich in organic matter, with perlite |
| Toxicity to pets | Non-toxic to cats, dogs and horses |

Most of these figures come from the [NC State Extension page for the peacock plant](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/goeppertia-makoyana/), one of the most common calatheas. Botanists have moved many species once called *Calathea*, including this one, to the genus *Goeppertia*, but shops still sell them as calatheas, and the rattlesnake plant, the zebra plant and *C. orbifolia* need much the same care. NC State describes the peacock plant as a rainforest plant from southeastern Brazil that grows in warm, shaded, humid conditions, and that is the setting to copy indoors.

## How much light does a calathea need?

NC State recommends bright, indirect sun or partial shade, and warns that direct sun burns the leaves while too little light makes their patterns fade. In practice:

- An east window, or a spot 1 to 2 m (3 to 6 ft) back from a south or west window, gives the right amount.
- A north window works if it is large and unobstructed.
- Bleached, papery or scorched patches on the leaves facing the window mean the sun is too strong. Move the plant back or add a sheer curtain.
- Dull, washed-out markings and slow growth mean the light is too weak.

Our [houseplant light guide](/en/blog/houseplant-light-guide) explains how to judge a window's strength by the shadows it casts.

## How often should you water a calathea?

NC State says the mix should be moist but not wet or soggy, and lists both wet soil and dry soil among the conditions calathea does not tolerate. That leaves a narrower window than for most houseplants. Water when the top 2 cm (¾ in) or so starts to feel dry, before the rest of the pot does. In a warm, bright room in summer that is often once a week; in winter it can be every 10 to 14 days. NC State advises watering less in winter.

Water slowly until water runs from the drainage hole, then empty the saucer after 15 minutes. Signs to watch:

- leaves curling or rolling inward and edges turning crisp while the mix is dry point to underwatering or dry air;
- yellowing lower leaves, drooping stems and a mix that stays wet for days point to overwatering. NC State notes that overwatering causes root rot.

Our guide on [how often to water a calathea](/en/blog/how-often-to-water-calathea) covers the seasonal schedule in detail.

## Does a calathea need filtered water?

Often, yes. NC State recommends distilled or rain water, because the fluoride in tap water can cause the leaves to brown. If your calathea gets brown tips and edges despite good humidity and steady watering, switch to rainwater, distilled water or water from a filter jug, and see whether the new leaves come in clean. Brown edges on old leaves will not recover; trim them along the leaf's outline with clean scissors if they bother you.

Water at room temperature. Cold water straight from the tap can chill a tropical plant's roots, and NC State lists cold drafts and inconsistent temperatures among calathea's intolerances.

## How do you raise humidity for a calathea?

NC State gives 60% as the humidity level to maintain, and notes that low humidity makes the leaves roll or brown. Heated homes in winter often sit well below that. What works:

- A humidifier near the plant is the most reliable way to reach 50 to 60%.
- Grouping plants together helps a little, because each one releases moisture.
- A bathroom or kitchen with a bright window is often more humid than a living room.
- Keep the plant away from radiators, heat vents and air conditioning, which dry the air around it.

Misting wets the leaves for a few minutes and does not raise room humidity for long. Our guide on [how to increase humidity for houseplants](/en/blog/how-to-increase-humidity-for-houseplants) compares the methods.

## What temperature does a calathea like?

NC State gives 18 to 24°C (65 to 75°F) and lists low or inconsistent temperatures and cold drafts as problems. The University of Arkansas Extension [table of houseplant temperatures](https://www.uaex.uada.edu/yard-garden/home-landscape/docs/Temperature%20Requirements%20of%20Selected%20House%20Plants.pdf) puts calathea among the high-temperature plants. Keep it away from doors that open in winter and from windows that get cold at night. A calathea left in a cold draft often reacts with curled, drooping leaves within a few days.

## What soil and pot does a calathea need?

NC State recommends a moist but well-drained potting mix high in organic matter, with perlite added to improve drainage. A good houseplant compost with about a quarter of perlite works. Use a pot with a drainage hole, since calathea does not tolerate wet soil, and choose one only slightly larger than the root ball. Our guide to the [best soil for houseplants](/en/blog/best-soil-for-houseplants) explains the mixes.

Repot in spring when roots fill the pot, which for most calatheas is every year or two. That is also the time to divide it.

## Should you fertilise a calathea?

NC State suggests a balanced fertiliser every 4 weeks during the growing season, and stopping in winter. Use a liquid houseplant fertiliser at the strength on the label or weaker. Too much fertiliser builds up salts in the mix, which can brown leaf edges the same way hard water does. If you see a white crust on the mix, flush the pot with plenty of water.

## How do you propagate a calathea?

By division. NC State names division of the rhizomatous roots in late spring as the preferred method, and suggests dividing every two years to keep the plant vigorous.

1. Water the day before so the root ball holds together.
2. Slide the plant out and look for natural clumps, each with its own stems and roots.
3. Pull or cut the clumps apart with a clean knife, keeping several leaves and a good set of roots on each.
4. Pot each one in fresh mix, water, and keep it warm and humid for a few weeks while it settles.

Calatheas do not root from leaf or stem cuttings the way pothos does.

## Why do calathea leaves move at night?

It is normal. NC State notes that the leaves fold up in the evening and reopen at sunrise in response to changes in light and temperature. That is where the family's common name, prayer plants, comes from. Leaves that stay folded or curled during the day are a different matter: check the humidity, the mix and the temperature.

## Pests, problems and pets

NC State lists aphids, scale, mealybugs and spider mites as pests to watch for. Spider mites are the ones to watch in dry winter air; look for fine pale speckling and webbing under the leaves. Check the undersides when you water. Our article on [calathea problems](/en/blog/calathea-problems) goes through brown edges, curling, yellowing and pests symptom by symptom.

The [ASPCA](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/animal-poison-control/toxic-and-non-toxic-plants/calathea) lists *Calathea* species as non-toxic to dogs, cats and horses, which makes calathea one of the safer patterned plants for a home with pets.
