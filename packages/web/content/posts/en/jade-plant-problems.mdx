Most jade plant problems come down to two things: watering and light. A jade (Crassula ovata) needs at least 4 hours of direct sun a day and soil that dries out between deep waterings, with even less water from autumn to late winter. Get those right and the dropped, wrinkled or soft leaves usually stop.

## Why is my jade plant dropping leaves?

Leaf drop is the symptom people search for most, and it has opposite causes. Squeeze a fallen leaf and check the soil before you change anything.

**1. Overwatering.** The most common cause indoors. [University of Wisconsin Extension](https://hort.extension.wisc.edu/articles/jade-plant-crassula-ovata/) notes that overwatered jades drop leaves and can develop stem rot. The fallen leaves are soft, swollen or translucent, often yellow, and the soil is still damp days after watering. Stop watering until the soil is dry all the way down, then check the stem base and roots as described in the root rot section.

**2. Underwatering.** Drought also makes jades drop leaves, according to Wisconsin, and Clemson Cooperative Extension says leaves fall if the plant is kept extremely dry. Here the leaves are thin, wrinkled and limp before they fall, and the soil is bone dry and pulling away from the pot. Water deeply until it drains from the hole. Our article on [how often to water a jade plant](/en/blog/how-often-to-water-jade-plant) gives starting intervals by season.

**3. Sudden change.** Moving a jade from a sunny window to a dim room, or from outdoors back inside in autumn, can trigger a few weeks of leaf drop while it adjusts.

**4. Cold.** Wisconsin warns that freezing kills jade plants. Leaves touching a frosty window, or a night outdoors in cold weather, turn dark and mushy and drop. Keep it well away from frost. Clemson suggests 18 to 24°C (65 to 75°F) during the day.

**How to tell overwatering from underwatering.** Soft, squishy, translucent leaves with wet soil point to too much water. Thin, wrinkled, rubbery leaves with dry soil point to too little. Our guide to [overwatering vs underwatering](/en/blog/overwatering-vs-underwatering) covers more signs.

## Why are my jade plant leaves wrinkled or shrivelled?

**1. Thirst.** A jade stores water in its leaves. When it runs low, the leaves shrink and wrinkle. If the soil is dry and the leaves plump up within a few days of a deep watering, that was the cause.

**2. Rotten roots.** If the leaves are wrinkled but the soil is wet, the roots may have rotted and can no longer take up water. Unpot it and check.

**3. Old leaves.** A few wrinkled lower leaves on an otherwise firm plant are just old leaves being used up. They fall on their own.

## Why are my jade plant leaves soft, mushy or yellow?

**1. Too much water.** Soft, yellowing leaves, especially lower ones, almost always mean the soil has stayed wet too long. Let it dry out completely before watering again, and from autumn to late winter water even less. [NC State Extension](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/crassula-ovata/) advises watering moderately when dry and reducing water through that period.

**2. Poor drainage.** A pot without a drainage hole, a cachepot holding water, or dense compost keeps the roots wet. Clemson lists poor drainage as the cause of root rot. Wisconsin recommends a succulent mix.

**3. Cold and wet together.** Wet soil in a cold room is the fastest way to turn leaves mushy.

## How do I know if my jade plant has root rot?

Look for a soft, dark or shrivelled stem at soil level, leaves dropping in wet soil, and a sour smell from the pot. Ease the plant out. Healthy roots are firm and pale. Rotten roots are brown or black and mushy, and they pull apart easily.

**To save it:**

1. Remove all the old soil and cut away every soft or dark root with clean scissors.
2. If the stem is rotten at the base, cut above the rot until the inside is clean and pale green.
3. Let the cut ends dry for a few days until they callus.
4. Pot it into dry succulent mix in a pot with a drainage hole.
5. Wait about a week before watering, then water only when the mix is dry.

If the whole stem is soft, take healthy stem tips or leaves as cuttings. NC State notes that jade grows from both leaf and stem cuttings. Our guide to [plant propagation from cuttings](/en/blog/plant-propagation-cuttings) covers the method, and our [root rot recovery](/en/blog/root-rot-recovery) article covers the repotting steps in more detail.

## Why is my jade plant leggy or droopy?

**1. Not enough light.** Wisconsin says jades in low light develop deep green leaves and drooping stems. Long gaps between leaves and stems that lean towards the window mean the plant is stretching for light. It needs at least 4 hours of direct sun. Clemson recommends a south-facing window.

**2. Top-heavy growth.** Old jades grow heavy, and branches can bend or snap under their own weight. Prune in spring or summer, cutting just above a leaf pair. New shoots grow from that point and the plant becomes bushier. The cut pieces root easily.

Move the plant to brighter light gradually over a week or two so the leaves do not scorch.

## Why does my jade plant have red edges or brown spots?

**Red edges.** Usually not a problem. NC State notes that the leaf margins turn red in bright sun. It is a normal response to strong light.

**Brown, dry patches.** Sunburn, after a sudden move into strong sun or outdoors. The damaged patches stay, but new growth will be fine once the plant is acclimatised.

**Black or dark soft spots.** Cold damage or rot. Remove the affected leaves and keep the plant warmer and drier.

## Jade plant problems caused by pests

NC State lists aphids, scale, spider mites and mealybugs.

- **Mealybugs.** The most common. White, cottony clusters in leaf joints, on stems and under leaves. Wisconsin recommends dabbing them with a cotton swab dipped in rubbing alcohol. Repeat every few days until no more appear.
- **Scale.** Small, brown, hard bumps on stems and leaves. Scrape them off or dab them with alcohol.
- **Spider mites.** Fine pale speckling and webbing, mostly in dry indoor air. Rinse the plant and check the undersides of the leaves.
- **Aphids.** Clusters on new growth. Rinse them off.

Be careful with sprays. Wisconsin warns that insecticides can damage jade, and Clemson notes that insecticidal soap may harm it, so test any product on a single leaf first and wait a few days. Our guide to [common houseplant pests](/en/blog/common-houseplant-pests) helps with identification.

## Why won't my jade plant flower?

An indoor jade often never blooms. According to Wisconsin, flowering usually follows several weeks of cool nights, dry soil and long nights without artificial light in autumn. If you want to try, keep it in a cool, bright room that stays dark at night from early autumn, and water sparingly.

## Quick answers

**Is a jade plant toxic to pets?** Yes. The [ASPCA](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/aspca-poison-control/toxic-and-non-toxic-plants/jade-plant) lists it as toxic to dogs, cats and horses, with vomiting, depression and incoordination as signs.

**Should I mist my jade plant?** No. It prefers dry air, and wet leaves can encourage rot.
