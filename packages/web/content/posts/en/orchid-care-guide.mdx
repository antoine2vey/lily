Orchid care for the common moth orchid (*Phalaenopsis*) comes down to bright light without hot sun, a thorough soak about once a week when it grows in bark, and a few weeks of cooler nights in autumn so it flowers again. It grows in chunky bark, not soil, and needs fresh bark every one to two years. Most moth orchids that die indoors are killed by water held around the roots, and most that never rebloom are kept too dark.

This guide covers *Phalaenopsis*, the orchid sold in supermarkets and garden centres. Other orchids such as *Cattleya*, *Dendrobium* and *Oncidium* need more light and different rest periods.

## Orchid care at a glance

| Need | What to give it |
|---|---|
| Light | An east window, or a shaded south or west window. No hot midday sun |
| Water | Soak thoroughly about once a week in bark; less often in sphagnum moss. Never let it sit in water |
| Humidity | 50% or more is ideal; a pebble tray with the pot above the water helps |
| Temperature | Days 21 to 27°C (70 to 80°F), nights 16 to 21°C (60 to 70°F); not below 10°C (50°F) |
| Potting mix | Medium-grade orchid bark in a pot with drainage holes, not soil |
| Feeding | Balanced fertiliser at half strength, weekly or every few waterings; less in winter |
| Toxicity | Non-toxic to cats, dogs and horses |

## How much light does an orchid need?

Moth orchids are low-light orchids, but low for an orchid still means a bright window. The [University of Maryland Extension](https://www.extension.umd.edu/resource/care-phalaenopsis-orchids-moth-orchids) recommends an east or west window. NC State Extension suggests an east window, or a west or south window that is well shaded. A sheer curtain softens strong afternoon sun.

The leaves are the best light meter. According to UMD:

- **Light, olive or medium green leaves:** the light is right.
- **Dark green leaves:** too little light. The plant may grow but will not flower.
- **Reddish leaf edges, or yellow, bleached patches:** too much light.

UMD names low light as the most common reason a moth orchid does not rebloom. If the only spot you have is a north window, a grow light helps.

## How often should you water an orchid?

In bark, a mature moth orchid usually needs a thorough watering about once a week. The [Missouri Botanical Garden](https://www.missouribotanicalgarden.org/Portals/0/Kemper%20Gardens/Fact%20Sheets/Orchids%20-%20Phalaenopsis%20%282020%29.pdf) gives that interval, and says sphagnum moss is watered less often because it holds water longer. In summer heat the plant may need water more often. In a cool winter room, less.

Check before you water. Roots inside a clear pot turn silvery white when they are dry and green when they are wet. You can also push a wooden skewer into the bark: if it comes out dry, water.

How to water:

1. Water in the morning with lukewarm water.
2. Take the pot to the sink and let water run through the bark for about a minute, or drench it 3 or 4 times over 10 minutes, as UMD suggests.
3. Let it drain completely before it goes back in its cachepot.
4. Pour away any water that collects in the crown, where the leaves meet. Water left there can rot the plant from the centre. A twist of paper towel soaks it up.

Two things to avoid. Do not use ice cubes: MoBot advises against them because they chill the roots and provide too little water. Do not use water from a salt-based water softener. ## Humidity and temperature

Moth orchids like humidity of 50% or more. A pebble tray helps, as long as the pot sits on the pebbles above the waterline and never in the water. UMD notes that misting does not raise humidity enough to matter, and water left on the leaves can cause spots. A humidifier or grouping plants does more.

MoBot gives days of 21 to 27°C (70 to 80°F) and nights of 16 to 21°C (60 to 70°F), with limits of about 35°C (95°F) and 10°C (50°F). Keep plants out of cold draughts in winter and away from radiators and hot, sunny glass in summer.

## How to get an orchid to rebloom

A moth orchid sets new flower spikes when nights cool down in autumn. MoBot says nights around 13°C (55°F) for several weeks in autumn start the spikes. UMD notes that evening temperatures in the 50s°F (10 to 15°C) prompt buds. A spot near a cool window, or a room without heating, through October and November usually does it.

Once a spike appears:

- Keep the plant in the same position so the buds open toward the light. Moving it can cause buds to drop.
- Support the spike with a stake and clip as it grows.
- Expect flowers to last 2 to 6 months.

When the flowers fall, you have two options. Cut the spike just above the second node below the old flowers, and it may branch and flower again, though MoBot notes this weakens the plant. Or cut the spike near its base, which lets the plant rest and build up for a stronger spike next year. Remove a spike at the base once it turns yellow or brown.

## Best potting mix and pot for an orchid

Moth orchids grow on trees in the wild. Their roots need air as much as water, so they grow in coarse bark, not potting soil. MoBot recommends medium-grade bark. Sphagnum moss also works, but it stays wet much longer and is easy to overwater.

Use a pot with plenty of drainage holes. Clear plastic orchid pots let you see the roots. Choose a pot that fits the roots snugly, because MoBot warns against overpotting. The silvery roots that grow over the pot rim are aerial roots. They are normal. Do not cut them off.

## When and how to repot an orchid

Repot every one to two years, or sooner if the bark breaks down into fine, soggy crumbs. The best time is after flowering, in spring or summer, when new roots or leaves appear. NC State gives about every two years.

1. Take the plant out and remove all the old bark.
2. Sort the roots. Healthy roots are firm, plump and whitish or green. Dead roots are brown and either mushy or papery and wiry. Cut off the dead ones with clean scissors.
3. Set the plant in a pot just big enough for the healthy roots, with the base of the lowest leaf at the surface.
4. Work fresh, pre-soaked bark between the roots and tap the pot to settle it.

## How to fertilise an orchid

NC State recommends a balanced fertiliser, either at half strength twice a month or at quarter strength every week. MoBot suggests a balanced 20-20-20 fertiliser at half strength weekly or three weeks out of four, cut to quarter strength in winter, and flushing the pot with plain water once a month to clear salts. UMD feeds every third or fourth watering and skips winter.

Any of these works. Pick one and stick to it. Older advice to use a high-nitrogen 30-10-10 formula is not needed for moth orchids grown in fresh bark. Our guide on [how to fertilise houseplants](/en/blog/how-to-fertilize-houseplants) explains label numbers and dilution.

## Common orchid problems

- **Wrinkled, limp leaves:** usually dead roots, from rot or long dryness. Check the roots before you water more.
- **Mushy brown roots and yellow lower leaves:** overwatering or old, broken-down bark. Repot.
- **Buds yellowing and falling before they open:** a sudden change of temperature, draughts, or moving the plant. NC State lists bud drop as a reaction to changes in conditions.
- **Brown spots on flowers:** grey mould (*Botrytis*), favoured by cool, damp, still air. Remove spotted flowers and improve airflow.
- **White cottony tufts or brown bumps on leaves:** mealybugs or scale. Wipe them off with a cotton swab dipped in rubbing alcohol.

## Is an orchid safe for cats and dogs?

Yes. The ASPCA and NC State both list *Phalaenopsis* as non-toxic to cats, dogs and horses. That makes it one of the better flowering plants for a home with pets. See our list of [plants safe for cats](/en/blog/plants-safe-for-cats) for more.
