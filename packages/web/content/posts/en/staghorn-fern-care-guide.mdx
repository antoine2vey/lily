Staghorn fern care is mostly about watering the right way: soak the whole mount or basket for 10 to 20 minutes when the moss is nearly dry, then let it drain completely. Give it bright, indirect light, keep it above 13°C (55°F), and leave the brown, shield-shaped fronds at the base alone, because they are part of a healthy plant. Overwatering, not dry air, is what kills most staghorns indoors.

## Staghorn fern care at a glance

| Need | What to give it |
|---|---|
| Light | Bright, indirect light or dappled shade. No direct midday sun |
| Water | Soak the mount or root ball when the moss is nearly dry; about weekly in warm weather, every 2 to 3 weeks in winter |
| Humidity | Moderate. Good air movement matters more than very high humidity |
| Temperature | Above 13°C (55°F). The common staghorn survives brief dips near freezing |
| Growing medium | Sphagnum moss on a wooden board, or an orchid bark and moss mix in a basket |
| Feeding | Balanced liquid fertiliser, diluted, monthly in spring and summer |
| Toxicity | Non-toxic to cats and dogs |

The common staghorn fern sold as a houseplant is Platycerium bifurcatum. It is an epiphyte: in the wild it clings to tree trunks and branches, with no soil at all. Its roots take in water from rain running down the bark and from debris trapped behind its fronds. Most of the care advice below follows from that.

## The two kinds of fronds

A staghorn makes two very different fronds, and knowing which is which saves a lot of worry.

- **Shield fronds** (basal fronds) are round and flat. They press against the board or basket, cover the root ball and catch falling leaves and water. [NC State Extension](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/platycerium-bifurcatum/) notes that they are sterile and often turn brown as they mature. New green shields grow over the old brown ones.
- **Fertile fronds** are the forked "antlers." They do the photosynthesis and carry brown spore patches on their undersides near the tips. NC State says they reach up to 1.2 m (4 ft), usually 60 to 90 cm (2 to 3 ft) in cultivation.

[University of Wisconsin Extension](https://hort.extension.wisc.edu/articles/staghorn-fern-platycerium-bifurcatum/) is clear that the brown shield fronds should not be removed, even if they look dead, until they fall off on their own. It also warns against wiping off the fine whitish-grey fuzz on the fertile fronds. Those hairs help the plant hold on to water.

## How much light does a staghorn fern need?

Bright, indirect light, like the dappled light under a tree canopy. Indoors that means near an east-facing window, or a little back from a south- or west-facing one with a sheer curtain. Direct midday sun bleaches and scorches the fronds.

Too little light causes as much trouble. In a dim room the plant grows slowly, the moss stays wet for longer, and rot and pests become more likely. A bathroom with a frosted window often works well, as long as it gets real daylight.

## How to water a staghorn fern

Wisconsin Extension advises soaking the shield fronds and the medium behind them, not only the edge of the moss.

The easiest method for a mounted fern:

1. Take the board down and lay it in a sink, bath or bucket of room-temperature water, fern side down, so the moss and shield fronds are submerged.
2. Leave it for 10 to 20 minutes, until the moss is heavy and no more bubbles rise.
3. Stand it upright to drain for 15 to 30 minutes before you hang it back up.

For a fern in a basket or pot, water slowly over the root ball and shield fronds until water runs through, or dunk the basket in the same way.

**When to water.** Wait until the moss feels almost dry and the mount is noticeably lighter. The University of Florida's UF/IFAS extension suggests waiting until the fertile fronds wilt very slightly, because the moss is hard to judge by eye. They give roughly once a week in warm, dry weather and less often in cool or cloudy weather. Indoors in winter that often stretches to every two to three weeks.

Rainwater is best if you can collect it. Wisconsin notes that established plants are fairly drought tolerant, and that they rot easily when overwatered. If in doubt, wait another day or two.

## Humidity: does a staghorn fern need misting?

A staghorn likes moderate humidity, but it does not need a rainforest. Wisconsin Extension says indoor humidity can be a challenge and suggests misting when the air is very dry, or growing it in a bathroom. Misting the fronds a few times a week in winter is reasonable, but it is a top-up, not a substitute for soaking. A humidifier in the room does more.

Watch the other side too. NC State notes that black spots can appear on the fronds when humidity is too high and the air is still. Keep air moving around the plant, and do not hang it in a sealed corner.

## Temperature, summer and winter care

Wisconsin Extension says staghorns do best at normal household temperatures above 13°C (55°F). UF/IFAS adds that most Platycerium species cannot tolerate temperatures below 13°C (55°F), but Platycerium bifurcatum can take brief dips to about -1°C (30°F). That is survival, not comfort, so do not test it.

**Summer.** Growth is fastest from late spring to late summer. The mount dries quicker, so check it every few days and expect to soak it about weekly. You can hang it outdoors in summer in a sheltered spot out of direct sun and wind. Bring it back in before nights fall below about 10°C (50°F); Wisconsin advises moving it in before nights reach the 40s °F (around 4 to 9°C).

**Winter.** Growth slows, so the mount stays wet much longer. Soak it only when the moss is nearly dry, which may be every two to three weeks. Keep it away from cold window panes, draughty doors and the direct heat of a radiator, which dries one side of the fern long before the other. Our guide to [houseplant care in winter](/en/blog/houseplant-care-in-winter) covers these adjustments.

## How to mount a staghorn fern

Mounting suits the plant better than a pot. You need a board of untreated wood or a slab of bark, a handful of soaked sphagnum moss and something to tie it with.

1. Fix a hook to the back of the board before you start.
2. Shape a mound of damp sphagnum moss in the centre of the board.
3. Set the fern's root ball on the moss, with the shield fronds against the board.
4. Tie it on with fishing line, nylon tights or plastic-coated wire wrapped through and over the brown shield fronds. Keep the ties off the green fertile fronds. UF/IFAS advises against copper wire.

Within a season or two, new shield fronds grow over the ties and hold the plant on by themselves.

## Feeding a staghorn fern

Wisconsin Extension recommends a balanced, diluted liquid fertiliser once a month during the warm months. Add it to the soaking water at half the label strength. UF/IFAS suggests monthly feeding for young ferns and only one or two feeds a year for large, mature ones. Do not feed in winter. Some growers tuck a banana peel behind the shield fronds; it adds little and attracts fruit flies indoors.

## Common staghorn fern problems

- **Brown shield fronds:** normal. They age to brown and stay on as part of the plant.
- **Black or dark spots spreading on the base of the fertile fronds, soft brown patches at the centre:** rot from water that stays too long. Let the moss dry further between soaks and improve air flow.
- **Fertile fronds wilting with dry moss:** thirst. Soak it. They recover within a day.
- **Pale, scorched fronds on one side:** too much direct sun.
- **Pests:** Wisconsin lists scale insects and mealybugs, and NC State adds spider mites and aphids. Check the undersides of the fertile fronds. Scale is easy to confuse with the brown spore patches near the frond tips: spores sit in a flat, velvety patch, scale insects are separate, raised bumps that you can lift off with a fingernail. Our guide to [common houseplant pests](/en/blog/common-houseplant-pests) covers treatment.

## How to propagate a staghorn fern

A healthy staghorn makes small offsets, called pups, at the edges and back of the mount. NC State lists dividing off these pups. Cut one free with a sharp knife so it keeps some green fronds, some shield fronds and roots. Mount it on its own small board with damp moss and keep it warm and lightly moist until it establishes, which can take a long time. Spores also work, but very slowly.

## Is a staghorn fern safe for pets?

Yes. The [ASPCA](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/aspca-poison-control/toxic-and-non-toxic-plants/common-staghorn-fern) lists Platycerium bifurcatum as non-toxic to dogs, cats and horses. Any plant can still upset a pet's stomach if a lot of it is eaten.
