The most common areca palm problems are brown leaf tips, yellowing lower fronds and spider mites. Brown tips usually mean dry air, uneven watering or too much fertiliser; yellowing that starts on the oldest fronds often points to a potassium shortage; and fine speckling with webbing means spider mites, which arecas are very prone to. Real diseases are rare indoors.

## Areca palm problems: start with water, air and the oldest fronds

[Clemson's indoor palms factsheet](https://hgic.clemson.edu/factsheet/indoor-palms/) sums up palm trouble in the home: diseases are very rarely a problem, and the main issues are too much or too little water, plus insects and mites. Before treating anything, check:

1. **The soil.** Clemson says palms should be kept moist, never allowed to dry out and never left standing in water. Is the soil bone dry, evenly moist, or soggy?
2. **Where the symptom starts.** On the oldest, lowest fronds, on new growth, or all over?
3. **The undersides of the leaflets.** Look for speckling, webbing, brown bumps or white fluff.

The areca palm (*Dypsis lutescens*, formerly *Chrysalidocarpus lutescens*) wants bright, indirect light near an east, west or south window, according to Clemson, and nights of 18 to 21°C (65 to 70°F) with days of 24 to 29°C (75 to 85°F).

## Why does my areca palm have brown tips?

**1. Dry air and dry soil.** Clemson lists dry air and lack of water as causes of browning, and says palms benefit from added humidity when indoor air is below 50%. Heated rooms in winter are often far below that. A humidifier works best. A tray of gravel and water under the pot also helps a little, as long as the pot sits above the water line. Misting, which Clemson also mentions, only raises humidity for a few minutes, so do not rely on it. Our [humidity guide](/en/blog/how-to-increase-humidity-for-houseplants) compares the options.

**2. Too much fertiliser.** Clemson notes that tips of lower leaves may turn brown and die from excessive fertilisation. It recommends a slow-release palm fertiliser only two to three times a year, during spring and summer growth. Salts from fertiliser and hard tap water build up in the pot; water thoroughly so some drains out each time, and flush the pot with plenty of water now and then.

**3. Letting the soil dry out.** Arecas do not store much water. A pot that dries out completely between waterings shows it first at the leaflet tips.

**What to do:** fix the cause first. Then trim brown tips with clean, sharp scissors, following the shape of the leaflet and leaving a thin line of brown so you do not cut into green tissue.

## Why are my areca palm fronds turning yellow?

**1. Potassium deficiency (older fronds first).** This is the most common nutritional problem in palms. The [University of Florida's guide to potassium deficiency in palms](https://edis.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/EP269) says symptoms always appear first on the oldest leaves. On areca palms they start as translucent yellow or orange spots on the leaflets, then the tips and margins die back, and the worst leaves end up brown and frizzled. Symptoms are strongest at the tip of each frond and on the oldest fronds, fading toward newer growth.

The fix is a palm fertiliser with potassium and magnesium, such as an 8-2-12 analysis with added magnesium, used regularly. UF warns that recovery can take one to two years or more, because the whole canopy has to be replaced by new healthy fronds. It also found that regularly cutting off the discoloured older fronds speeds up the decline. Leave yellowing fronds on until they are completely brown, because the palm is still pulling nutrients from them.

**2. Overwatering.** Yellowing that spreads across the plant, with soil that stays wet and a sour smell, points to root rot. Clemson says root rot usually comes from a mix that drains slowly or from watering too often.

**3. Underwatering.** Yellowing combined with crispy brown tips on soil that has gone completely dry.

**4. Old age.** Each stem of an areca carries a limited number of fronds. One old, low frond yellowing at a time while the rest of the palm is green is normal.

**5. Too much direct sun.** Pale, washed-out or yellow-bleached fronds facing a sunny window. Move the palm a little back from the glass.

## Why does my areca palm have brown spots or patches?

**1. Cold injury.** Clemson describes reddish-brown dead areas on leaves that appear a few days after a chill, and says most houseplant palms need temperatures above 7°C (45°F) to avoid chilling damage. A palm bought in winter and carried home unwrapped, or one pressed against a cold window, often shows this.

**2. Potassium deficiency.** The necrotic spotting described above, on older fronds.

**3. Leaf shine products.** Clemson warns that leaf shine products can severely injure palm foliage. Clean the leaves with a damp cloth or a lukewarm shower instead.

## Why is my areca palm drooping?

**1. Thirst.** Fronds that sag and fold, with dry soil, need a thorough watering. They usually recover within a day.

**2. Root rot.** Drooping with wet soil and stems that turn soft and brown at the base. Unpot the plant: healthy roots are firm and light-coloured, rotten ones are dark and mushy. Cut away rotten roots, repot into fresh, porous mix and water less often. Our [root rot recovery guide](/en/blog/root-rot-recovery) has the steps.

**3. Cramped roots.** NC State Extension suggests repotting arecas every 2 or 3 years. A palm that dries out within a day or two of watering and has roots pushing out of the bottom needs a bigger pot.

## What pests attack areca palms?

**Spider mites.** Clemson calls the areca very susceptible to spider mite infestations, and notes mites are common indoors because of low humidity and warm temperatures. Look for fine pale or yellow speckling on the leaflets, a dull, dusty look, and very fine webbing where the leaflets join the stem. Hold a sheet of white paper under a frond and tap it; moving specks are mites. Rinse the whole palm in the shower, paying attention to the undersides, then treat with insecticidal soap every 5 to 7 days for several weeks. Raising humidity makes the palm less hospitable to them. Our [spider mite treatment guide](/en/blog/spider-mites-treatment) has the full routine.

**Scale insects.** Small brown or tan bumps along the stems and the undersides of leaflets, often with sticky honeydew below. Scrape them off and treat repeatedly.

**Mealybugs.** White, cottony clusters in the leaf joints and frond bases. Dab them with rubbing alcohol on a cotton swab.

**Whiteflies.** NC State also lists them. Tiny white insects that flutter up when the plant is disturbed.

## Why is my areca palm getting too tall?

Arecas grow 15 to 25 cm (6 to 10 in) a year and reach about 2 m (6 to 7 ft) indoors, according to Clemson, so they often outgrow their spot. Palms grow from a single growing point at the top of each stem, and Clemson notes they cannot be pruned back to reduce their height. Cutting the top off a stem kills that stem. To control size, remove whole stems at the base, or divide a crowded clump when you repot in spring.

## How to keep an areca palm healthy

- Bright, indirect light near an east, west or south window.
- Soil kept evenly moist, never dry, never waterlogged. Water thoroughly when the surface dries and empty the saucer. See [how often to water an areca palm](/en/blog/how-often-to-water-areca-palm).
- Humidity above 40 to 50% if you can manage it, especially in winter.
- A slow-release palm fertiliser two or three times a year in spring and summer, not general houseplant food alone. Our [fertiliser guide](/en/blog/how-to-fertilize-houseplants) explains the numbers on the label.
- Warmth: never below 7°C (45°F), and well away from cold windows and doors.

The areca palm is non-toxic to cats and dogs, according to the [ASPCA](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/animal-poison-control/toxic-and-non-toxic-plants/areca-palm).
