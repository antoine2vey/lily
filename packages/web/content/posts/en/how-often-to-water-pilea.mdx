How often to water pilea depends on the soil, not the calendar: water when the top 2.5 cm (1 in) has dried, which in a bright room usually means roughly once a week in spring and summer and every 2 to 3 weeks in winter. Pilea peperomioides, the Chinese money plant, has fleshy stems and shallow roots, and [NC State Extension](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/pilea-peperomioides/) notes that it is susceptible to root rot when overwatered. Erring slightly dry is safer than erring wet.

## When to water a pilea

Push a finger into the soil up to the first knuckle, about 2.5 cm (1 in). If it feels dry, water. If it still feels cool and damp, wait a few days and check again. Two other cues help:

- **Weight.** Lift the pot just after watering, then again a week later. A dry pot is noticeably lighter, and after a few rounds you can tell by feel.
- **The leaves.** A thirsty pilea lets its leaves droop and feel softer on their stalks. They firm up within a few hours of watering. Do not wait for this every time, but it is a reliable sign that you have waited long enough.

When you water, water thoroughly: pour slowly over the whole surface until water runs from the drainage hole, then empty the saucer after 15 minutes. A pot that sits in water keeps the bottom of the root ball soaked, which is where rot starts.

## How often to water pilea in each season

These are starting points for a pilea in a 12 to 15 cm (5 to 6 in) plastic pot near a bright window. Adjust them to what the soil tells you.

| Season | Starting interval | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Spring | Every 7 to 10 days | Days lengthen and growth picks up |
| Summer | Every 5 to 10 days | Warmth and long days dry the soil fastest |
| Autumn | Every 10 to 14 days | Light drops, growth slows |
| Winter | Every 2 to 3 weeks | Short days; the plant uses little water |

The switch between seasons is where most pileas get overwatered. In October the soil may stay damp for twice as long as it did in July, but a habit of weekly watering carries on. Check the soil before every watering for the first few weeks of autumn and reset your rhythm.

## What changes how often a pilea needs water

- **Light.** The single biggest factor. A pilea right beside a bright window uses far more water than one 2 m (6 ft) back in the room. NC State lists bright, indirect light as its preference; in dim light it grows leggy and the soil stays wet for longer.
- **Pot material.** Terracotta breathes and dries a few days sooner than plastic or glazed ceramic.
- **Pot size.** A small pot packed with roots dries quickly. A pot much larger than the root ball holds wet soil the roots cannot reach, which is a common cause of rot. Repot only one size up.
- **Soil.** NC State recommends a moist, well-drained potting mix. A dense, peaty mix stays wet longer; adding a handful of perlite per litre speeds drying. Our guide to the [best soil for houseplants](/en/blog/best-soil-for-houseplants) explains mixes.
- **Temperature and heating.** A warm room with radiators on dries the soil faster in winter than you might expect, so keep checking.

## Does pilea need distilled or rain water?

Probably not. Old posts often claim that pilea is sensitive to chlorine or fluoride in tap water. NC State's profile of the plant says nothing about water quality, and ordinary tap water at room temperature works for most homes.

The one real tap-water issue is hardness. In hard-water areas, minerals can build up as a white crust on the soil surface and the rim of the pot. If you see it, scrape off the crust and, every few months, run plain water through the pot for a minute at the sink to flush out the salts. Rainwater or filtered water is a fine choice if you already collect it, but it is not necessary.

## Signs of overwatering a pilea

- Lower leaves turning yellow and dropping, several at once.
- Leaves drooping while the soil is still wet.
- Black or brown, soft spots on leaves or at the base of leaf stalks.
- The main stem turning soft or dark at the soil line.
- Soil that smells sour, or fungus gnats flying up when you water.

If the stem is still firm, let the soil dry out more than usual before the next watering, and check the drainage hole is clear. If the stem is soft at the base, unpot the plant and look at the roots: healthy ones are pale and firm, rotten ones brown and mushy. Trim the rotten roots and repot into fresh, dry mix. If the stem itself is rotting, cut off the healthy top and root it as a cutting. Our [root rot recovery guide](/en/blog/root-rot-recovery) walks through it.

## Signs of underwatering a pilea

- Leaves droop and feel soft, then recover within hours of watering.
- Leaves curl inward or dome, and the edges turn brown and crisp.
- The soil has shrunk away from the sides of the pot.

Underwatering is easier to fix. Water slowly until it drains, wait a minute, and water again so the dry root ball absorbs it. If water runs straight through, stand the pot in a bowl of water for 20 to 30 minutes, then let it drain. Our guide to [overwatering versus underwatering](/en/blog/overwatering-vs-underwatering) compares the two sets of symptoms side by side.

Curling, spotted or yellowing leaves have causes other than water too, such as light, cold draughts and pests. Our [pilea care guide](/en/blog/pilea-care-guide) covers the rest of its needs.

## Quick answers

**Should I mist my pilea?** No need. NC State says it tolerates the humidity of an average home, and wetting the foliage can encourage leaf spots.

**Should I bottom-water a pilea?** You can. Stand the pot in 2 to 3 cm (1 in) of water for 20 to 30 minutes, then drain it. Water from the top every few waterings too, to flush minerals out of the soil.

**How do I water a pilea in a pot without drainage holes?** Move it into a pot with a hole, and use the decorative pot as an outer sleeve that you empty after watering. Without drainage, excess water has nowhere to go.
