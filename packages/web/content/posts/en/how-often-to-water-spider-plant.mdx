How often to water a spider plant comes down to one check: water when the top 2 to 3 cm (1 in) of soil is dry. In a typical room that is about once a week in spring and summer and every 10 to 14 days in winter. The plant stores water in thick roots and survives a missed watering; soil that stays wet is what causes trouble.

## How often to water a spider plant through the year

[Clemson Extension](https://hgic.clemson.edu/factsheet/spider-plant/) says spider plants should be allowed to dry out briefly between waterings. [NC State Extension](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/chlorophytum-comosum/) says the plant grows best in moist soil but tolerates drought and dry soil, and advises reducing watering in winter.

These starting intervals are our own estimate for a plant in a plastic pot with a drainage hole, in ordinary potting mix, in a room at 18 to 24°C (65 to 75°F). Treat them as the day to check the soil, not the day to water.

| Season | Starting interval | What is going on |
|---|---|---|
| Spring | About every 7 days | New leaves and runners; water use climbs |
| Summer | Every 5 to 7 days, sooner in a hanging basket or a warm room | Fastest drying of the year |
| Autumn | Every 7 to 10 days | Growth slows as days shorten |
| Winter | Every 10 to 14 days | Little growth; NC State says water less |

A spider plant that has filled its pot with roots can dry out in two or three days in summer. That is a sign it needs a bigger pot or dividing, not more frequent watering.

## How to tell when a spider plant needs water

- **Finger test.** Push a finger into the soil up to the first knuckle. Dry at the tip means water; cool and damp means wait.
- **Weight.** Lift the pot, or push up on the bottom of a hanging basket. A dry pot is noticeably lighter.
- **The leaves.** A thirsty spider plant's leaves lose their firmness and turn a duller, paler green. They recover quickly after watering, but do not use this as your routine signal; repeated dry spells brown the tips.
- **Clemson's soil checks.** The [watering factsheet](https://hgic.clemson.edu/factsheet/indoor-plants-watering/) adds a tap test for clay pots (hollow means dry) and the colour of the soil, which goes lighter as it dries.

## How to water a spider plant

Water slowly over the whole soil surface until water runs out of the drainage hole. Wait a few minutes, then empty the saucer or the cache-pot. Never leave the pot standing in water. Clemson says root rot usually comes from a soil mix that does not drain quickly or from excessive watering.

For hanging baskets, take the plant down and water it in the sink or shower, so it drains fully and you can see how much it drank.

If the soil has dried out so much that water runs straight down the sides, stand the pot in a bowl of water for 20 to 30 minutes, then let it drain.

## Which water to use

Spider plants are sensitive to what is in the water. NC State advises avoiding tap water because the chlorine and fluorides can cause leaf tip burn, and Clemson attributes tip burn to too much fertiliser or water high in soluble salts.

- **Rainwater or distilled water** is the safest choice if your plant keeps getting brown tips.
- **Tap water left to stand overnight** loses some chlorine, but the fluoride and dissolved salts stay.
- **Softened water** adds sodium; Clemson's watering factsheet advises against it for houseplants.
- **Room-temperature water** avoids a cold shock to the roots.

Whatever you use, water heavily enough that some drains out each time, and flush the pot with several pot-volumes of water every few months to wash out salts.

## What changes how often you water

**Light.** NC State says the plant prefers medium light and tolerates deep shade, but is intolerant of direct sun. A plant in a bright spot dries much faster than one in a dim corner.

**Temperature.** Clemson gives 18 to 24°C (65 to 75°F) by day and 10 to 13°C (50 to 55°F) at night as the preferred range, and suggests moving the plant a few feet from cold windows in winter. A warm room dries the soil faster; a cool one slows it down.

**Pot and basket.** Hanging baskets dry out faster than pots on a shelf, because warm air rises and baskets are exposed on all sides. Terracotta dries faster than plastic. See our guide to [choosing a pot](/en/blog/how-to-choose-a-pot-for-your-plant).

**Root mass.** Spider plants have thick, white, fleshy roots that store water and fill a pot quickly. Clemson advises dividing and repotting before the roots crack the container. A crowded pot holds little soil and dries out fast.

**Humidity.** NC State recommends medium humidity and suggests a humidifier or a tray of pebbles and water. Dry heated air in winter speeds up drying a little, even as growth slows.

## Signs you are overwatering a spider plant

- Leaves yellowing, often from the base, and going limp and soft.
- A soft, brown base where the leaves meet the soil.
- Soil that is still wet several days after watering, or smells sour.
- Brown, mushy roots instead of firm, white ones when you unpot the plant.

Stop watering, empty any standing water and let the top half of the soil dry. If the base is soft, unpot the plant, cut away mushy roots and repot in fresh, free-draining mix. Our [root rot recovery guide](/en/blog/root-rot-recovery) has the steps.

## Signs you are underwatering a spider plant

- Leaves that go limp, pale and dull, with dry, light soil.
- Brown, crisp tips after repeated dry spells. Clemson lists excessively dry soil among the causes of brown tips.
- Soil pulled away from the sides of the pot.
- Fewer runners and plantlets.

A spider plant usually firms up within a day of a thorough watering. Brown tips will not turn green again; trim them at an angle if you like. ## A simple routine

Check the soil on your chosen day. If the top 2 to 3 cm (1 in) is dry, water until it drains and empty the saucer. If not, check again in two or three days.
