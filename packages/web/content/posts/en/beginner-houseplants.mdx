The best beginner houseplants are the ones that put up with low light, dry soil and dry indoor air: pothos, snake plant, ZZ plant, spider plant, heartleaf philodendron, peace lily, cast iron plant, peperomia and dragon tree. All nine appear on [Iowa State University Extension's list of easy, low-maintenance houseplants](https://yardandgarden.extension.iastate.edu/how-to/easy-low-maintenance-houseplants), chosen because they tolerate a wide range of light, inconsistent watering and low humidity. Most of them need water only every 1 to 3 weeks, and the most common way to lose one is watering too often, not too little.

## Beginner houseplants at a glance

| Plant | Light | Water | Safe for cats and dogs? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pothos | Low to moderate | When the soil is moderately dry | No |
| Snake plant | Low to direct | When the soil is dry | No |
| ZZ plant | Low to indirect | When the soil is dry; tolerates drought | No |
| Spider plant | Indirect | Average; keep lightly moist | Yes |
| Heartleaf philodendron | Low to indirect | Keep evenly moist | No |
| Peace lily | Low; flowers best in indirect light | Keep moist; wilts when dry | No |
| Cast iron plant | Low to shade | Evenly moist, well drained | Yes |
| Peperomia | Bright indirect | When the soil is moderately dry | Yes |
| Dragon tree | Moderate to bright indirect | When the soil is dry, not bone dry | No |

Light and water come from Iowa State unless noted. Pet safety comes from the [ASPCA](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/animal-poison-control/toxic-and-non-toxic-plants) toxic and non-toxic plant lists, and for the ZZ plant from NC State Extension.

## What makes a plant good for beginners?

Iowa State uses five criteria, and they are a good test for any plant you see in a shop:

- it tolerates a wide range of light, especially low light;
- it survives inconsistent watering, and in particular dry soil;
- it looks good in low humidity;
- it stays healthy with minimal care, without messy leaf drop or special fertilisers;
- if it is grown for flowers, it blooms easily.

Plants that fail these tests are not bad plants, just harder first plants. A fern that needs constantly moist soil and humid air, for example, is a better second or third plant.

## 1. Pothos

Pothos (Epipremnum aureum) is a trailing vine with heart-shaped leaves, green or variegated with yellow or white. Iowa State says it grows in low to moderate light and should dry moderately between waterings. Pinch the tips to keep it full, and root the cuttings in water to make new plants. Variegated forms keep their colour better in brighter light. Our [pothos care guide](/en/blog/pothos-care-guide) covers it in detail.

## 2. Snake plant

The snake plant (Dracaena trifasciata, long sold as Sansevieria) has stiff, upright leaves and copes with anything from a dim corner to direct sun. Let the soil dry between waterings. Iowa State is blunt about it: the only way to kill this plant is to overwater it. In winter that can mean watering once a month or less. See our [snake plant care guide](/en/blog/snake-plant-care-guide).

## 3. ZZ plant

The ZZ plant (Zamioculcas zamiifolia) stores water in thick underground rhizomes, which is why it handles dry soil so well. Iowa State says it does exceptionally well in low light and tolerates dry conditions and low humidity. NC State Extension's plant database suggests watering about twice a month in summer and once a month in winter, only when the soil is dry. NC State rates it as moderately toxic to cats and dogs.

## 4. Spider plant

The spider plant (Chlorophytum comosum) has arching striped leaves and sends out small plantlets on long stems. Iowa State says it prefers indirect light and average moisture and temperatures. The plantlets root easily in a small pot of soil. It is one of the few easy plants that the ASPCA lists as non-toxic to cats and dogs.

## 5. Heartleaf philodendron

Heartleaf philodendron (Philodendron hederaceum) looks like pothos, with softer, thinner, fully heart-shaped leaves. It grows in low to indirect light. Unlike most plants here, Iowa State says to keep it evenly moist, so it suits someone who tends to water more often. Wash the leaves occasionally to remove dust, and cut back long stems to make it branch.

## 6. Peace lily

The peace lily (Spathiphyllum) grows in low light but flowers best in bright indirect light. Keep the soil moist. It is easy for beginners because it tells you when it is thirsty: Iowa State notes that the leaves wilt when the plant is dry and revive after watering. Water when it starts to droop, before the leaves go limp, and it recovers within hours. The ASPCA lists it as toxic to cats and dogs.

## 7. Cast iron plant

The cast iron plant (Aspidistra elatior) earned its name. Iowa State says it endures heat, dust, low light and lack of water better than most houseplants. It grows slowly, so buy a plant the size you want. Repot it rarely, because Iowa State notes frequent repotting harms it. It is non-toxic to cats and dogs according to the ASPCA, which makes it a good choice for a dark hallway in a home with pets.

## 8. Peperomia

Peperomias are small, compact plants with thick, often patterned leaves. Iowa State says to let the soil dry moderately between waterings, and warns that they tolerate neglect but rot if overwatered. Not every peperomia is equally easy: the thick-leaved Peperomia obtusifolia is the most forgiving, while thin-leaved types dry out faster. The ASPCA lists P. obtusifolia as non-toxic to pets.

## 9. Dragon tree

The dragon tree (Dracaena marginata) has thin, red-edged leaves on woody stems and slowly grows into a small indoor tree. Iowa State recommends moderate to indirect light: it tolerates low light, but bends and stretches toward the window. It prefers warm to average temperatures and does fine in below-average humidity. [NC State Extension](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/dracaena-marginata/) advises letting it dry between waterings, but not severely, and notes that it is sensitive to fluoride, which causes brown leaf tips. The ASPCA lists it as toxic to cats and dogs.

## How to choose beginner houseplants for your home

Start with your light, not the plant. Stand where the plant will go at midday. If you can read comfortably without a lamp, most plants on this list will do well there. If the spot is dim, pick from the low-light end: ZZ plant, snake plant, cast iron plant, pothos or heartleaf philodendron. Our [houseplant light guide](/en/blog/houseplant-light-guide) shows how to judge a window.

Then match the plant to your habits:

- **You forget to water:** snake plant, ZZ plant, peperomia. They store water and prefer to dry out.
- **You like to water often:** heartleaf philodendron, peace lily, spider plant. They prefer evenly moist soil, though still not soggy.
- **You have cats or dogs:** spider plant, cast iron plant, peperomia. Our list of [plants safe for cats](/en/blog/plants-safe-for-cats) has more options.

## How do you keep your first houseplant alive?

- **Use a pot with a drainage hole.** Most beginner losses are roots sitting in water. A decorative pot without a hole is fine as an outer cover, but tip out the water after each watering.
- **Check the soil before watering.** Push a finger into the soil. Water the dry-loving plants when it is dry a few centimetres down, and the moisture-loving ones when the surface feels dry. Our guide to [overwatering vs underwatering](/en/blog/overwatering-vs-underwatering) shows how to read the signs.
- **Water less in winter.** Plants grow slowly when days are short and the soil stays wet much longer.
- **Do not repot right away.** A new plant has enough to cope with in its new home. Wait until spring, or until roots come out of the bottom of the pot.
- **Expect a few yellow leaves at first.** Plants grown in bright greenhouses often drop a leaf or two as they adjust to a darker home.

Our page on [common houseplant care mistakes](/en/blog/houseplant-care-mistakes) covers the rest. If remembering is the hard part, [Lily](/en) sets a watering schedule for each plant on your iPhone and adjusts it for shorter winter days.
