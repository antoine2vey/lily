How often to water a peperomia depends on the soil, not the calendar: water when the top half of the mix has dried. In a bright room that often means every 1 to 2 weeks in spring and summer and every 3 to 4 weeks in winter. Peperomias store water in thick leaves and stems, so too much water kills far more of them than too little.

## How do you tell when a peperomia needs water?

[Clemson Extension](https://hgic.clemson.edu/factsheet/peperomia/) says peperomia prefers to dry out between waterings. Most peperomias sold as houseplants, such as the baby rubber plant (*Peperomia obtusifolia*), watermelon peperomia and ripple peperomia, have fleshy leaves that hold a reserve of water, so a short dry spell does them no harm.

Check before you water:

1. **Push a finger or a wooden skewer into the mix.** In a small pot of 10 to 12 cm (4 to 5 in), water when it is dry about halfway down. In a larger pot, when the top 5 cm (2 in) is dry.
2. **Lift the pot.** A dry peperomia pot feels noticeably light. After a few waterings you can judge it by weight alone.
3. **Look at the leaves.** Thick-leaved types turn slightly soft and less glossy when they need water. They firm up again within a day of watering.

When it is time, water slowly until water runs from the drainage hole. Clemson recommends letting the pot drain fully before you put it back in its saucer, so the roots never sit in water.

## How often should you water a peperomia in each season?

These are starting points for a plant in bright, indirect light in a heated home. Adjust them by checking the mix.

| Season | Starting interval |
|---|---|
| Spring | Every 1 to 2 weeks as growth resumes |
| Summer | Every 7 to 10 days in a warm, bright room |
| Autumn | Every 2 to 3 weeks as light falls |
| Winter | Every 3 to 4 weeks, sometimes less |

Peperomias grow mostly in spring and summer, which is also when Clemson advises feeding them. In winter, with less light, growth slows and the mix stays damp much longer. That is when most peperomias are overwatered: a plant that needed water every week in July may need it once a month in January.

## What changes how often a peperomia needs water?

- **Light.** [NC State Extension](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/peperomia-obtusifolia/) notes that peperomia prefers bright, indirect light but tolerates low light for months. In low light it uses very little water, so the gap between waterings should grow, sometimes to four or five weeks.
- **Pot size and material.** Peperomias have small root systems. Clemson suggests a pot only slightly larger than the nursery pot, and says the plant can stay in the same pot for several years. A pot that is too big holds water the roots cannot reach. Terracotta dries faster than plastic or glazed ceramic.
- **Soil.** Clemson recommends a well-drained houseplant or cactus mix. A dense, peaty mix can stay wet for two weeks; adding a third of perlite or fine bark makes it dry in a few days. Our article on the [best soil for houseplants](/en/blog/best-soil-for-houseplants) explains the options.
- **Temperature and air.** A warm room near a heater dries the pot faster than a cool one. NC State lists drafts among the conditions peperomia does not tolerate, so keep it away from cold windows and doors in winter.
- **Leaf type.** Thick, succulent-leaved peperomias go longer between waterings than thin-leaved trailing types such as cupid peperomia.

## What are the signs of an overwatered peperomia?

NC State notes that peperomia does not tolerate wet soil, and that overwatering yellows the leaves and leads to root rot. Clemson adds that an overwatered peperomia drops its leaves.

Look for:

- leaves turning yellow, often starting at the bottom;
- leaves falling off when touched, even though the mix is damp;
- stems that are soft, dark or mushy at the soil line;
- leaves that are limp or wilted while the mix is still wet, because rotting roots cannot take up water;
- small black flies around the pot (fungus gnats, which breed in wet mix).

What to do:

1. Stop watering and let the mix dry out until the top half is dry.
2. If the plant keeps declining, slide it out of its pot. Healthy roots are white or pale and firm; rotten ones are brown, soft and smell sour.
3. Cut off rotten roots and any mushy stems, and repot in fresh, fast-draining mix in a pot with a drainage hole, only slightly larger than the root ball.
4. Water lightly once, then wait until the top half is dry again.

If most of the roots are gone, take healthy stem tips as cuttings. Our [root rot recovery guide](/en/blog/root-rot-recovery) covers severe cases step by step.

## What are the signs of an underwatered peperomia?

NC State also lists very dry soil among the conditions peperomia dislikes. Underwatering is less common and easier to fix.

Look for:

- thick leaves that are soft, wrinkled or puckered, on firm stems;
- leaves curling inward, or drooping on thin-leaved types;
- dry, crispy brown leaf edges after a long dry spell;
- mix that has shrunk away from the side of the pot.

Water slowly until it drains, and if the mix has shrunk and water runs straight down the sides, stand the pot in a bowl of water for 20 minutes so the root ball soaks up evenly. The leaves should firm up within a day or two. If you are unsure which problem you have, our guide to [overwatering vs underwatering](/en/blog/overwatering-vs-underwatering) compares the two side by side.

## Should you mist a peperomia?

Not to water it. NC State says peperomia prefers medium to low humidity, so ordinary room air is fine. Clemson notes that a humidifier helps in winter, when heating makes the air very dry. Misting wets the leaves for a few minutes and does nothing for the roots.

[Lily](/en) schedules watering per plant and stretches the interval as winter daylight shortens, which is when a peperomia's water needs drop the most.

## Quick answers

**Should I water a peperomia from the bottom?** You can. Stand the pot in 2 to 3 cm (1 in) of water for 15 to 20 minutes, then let it drain. It is a good way to rewet a mix that has dried hard. Water from the top every few months to flush out fertiliser salts.

**Can I use tap water?** Usually, yes. If your water is very hard and you see a white crust on the mix, flush the pot with plenty of water every few months.
