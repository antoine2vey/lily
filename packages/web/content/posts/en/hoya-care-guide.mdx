Hoya care is mostly about light and restraint: give the plant bright light, including a few hours of gentle direct sun if you can, let a loose, fast-draining mix dry almost completely between waterings, and keep winter nights above 10 °C (50°F). Hoyas are epiphytes that grow on trees in the wild, so their roots want air as much as water. A hoya that gets enough light will usually start flowering at 2 to 3 years old, and the same flower spurs bloom again for years if you leave them on.

## Hoya care at a glance

| Need | What it wants |
|---|---|
| Light | Bright. Indirect light at minimum; a few hours of morning or filtered sun helps flowering |
| Water | When the mix is nearly dry all the way down. Every 1 to 2 weeks in summer, every 2 to 4 weeks in winter as a starting point |
| Humidity | Average home humidity is fine; higher is better. Use a humidifier, not misting |
| Temperature | Normal room temperature. Winter nights down to 10 °C (50°F), not lower |
| Soil | Loose, fast-draining mix, for example 1 part potting mix, 1 part orchid bark, 1 part perlite, in a pot with a hole |
| Repotting | Only when pot-bound and not in bud; the new pot no more than 5 cm (2 in) wider |
| Toxicity | Non-toxic to cats and dogs |

These figures come from the [NC State Extension page for Hoya carnosa](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/hoya-carnosa/) and the [Missouri Botanical Garden](https://www.missouribotanicalgarden.org/PlantFinder/PlantFinderDetails.aspx?taxonid=276833). Hoya carnosa, the wax plant, is the species most people own, and the advice below fits most of the common hoyas sold as houseplants.

## How much light does a hoya need?

More than most trailing plants. NC State recommends bright indirect light all year. The Missouri Botanical Garden goes further and suggests bright light with at least half a day of direct sun, while noting that the plant also accepts sun filtered through a curtain. Both agree that good light is what makes a hoya flower.

A practical spot is right in an east window, near a west window, or a little back from a south window behind a sheer curtain. Hot midday sun through glass can turn the leaves yellow or red and leave bleached patches. A hoya kept in a dim corner will survive and grow long bare stems between small leaves, but it will rarely bloom.

## How often should you water a hoya?

Water when the mix is nearly dry all the way through, then soak it until water drains from the bottom. NC State says to let the soil dry out between waterings and warns that soil that is either too wet or too dry makes the leaves drop. It also notes that cold water can shock the plant, so use water at room temperature.

As a starting point, that means every 1 to 2 weeks in a bright room in summer and every 2 to 4 weeks in winter, when the plant needs less water and less light. Thick, succulent-leaved hoyas such as H. carnosa and H. kerrii tolerate drying out better than thin-leaved species. Two quick checks help: push a finger or a wooden skewer into the mix, and lift the pot. A dry pot is noticeably lighter. Slightly soft, wrinkled leaves mean it is time to water; yellow leaves on wet soil mean you are watering too often.

[Lily](/en) can schedule watering for each plant and lengthen the interval as winter days shorten, which is when hoyas sit wet for the longest.

## What soil is best for a hoya?

A mix that drains in seconds and lets air reach the roots. NC State recommends a loose, fast-draining potting mix high in organic matter and a container with good drainage. A simple recipe is equal parts potting mix, orchid bark and perlite. Many growers also use a cactus mix with extra bark.

Hoyas like being snug in their pots. NC State advises repotting only when the plant has outgrown its container, never while it is in bud, and into a pot no more than 5 cm (2 in) larger. A plant that has filled its pot with roots often flowers more than one that has just been moved up. ## Do hoyas need high humidity?

They like it, but they do not need a greenhouse. NC State notes that the plant loves high humidity and suggests a humidifier or a tray of wet pebbles under the pot. The idea that hoyas need 60 to 80% humidity and daily misting is not true for the common species. Misting wets the leaves for a few minutes and does not change the air around the plant.

Average indoor humidity works for H. carnosa and most thick-leaved hoyas. Thin-leaved species are fussier, and very dry winter air can invite spider mites, which NC State lists as a problem in dry conditions.

## How do you get a hoya to flower?

Most hoyas need three things to bloom:

1. **Age.** NC State notes that most plants do not flower until they are 2 to 3 years old.
2. **Light.** This is the usual missing piece. Move a non-flowering plant to the brightest spot you have, with some morning sun.
3. **Undisturbed spurs.** Hoyas flower from short leafless stalks called peduncles or spurs. Do not cut them off after the flowers fade. The Missouri Botanical Garden and NC State both point out that new flowers form on the same spurs, year after year.

Once buds appear, the Missouri Botanical Garden advises against turning or moving the pot, because buds can drop when the light direction changes. H. carnosa flowers come in round clusters of 10 to 30 fragrant, star-shaped flowers, mostly in summer.

## How should you prune a hoya?

Lightly, and in early spring. Trim long bare stems to shape the plant, cutting just above a leaf node, and leave every flower spur in place. Long leafless tendrils at the end of a stem are new growth that will leaf out, so do not cut them unless the plant has run out of space. The cuttings root easily: NC State recommends propagation by stem cuttings. Our guide to [propagating plants from cuttings](/en/blog/plant-propagation-cuttings) covers the method.

## What about variegated hoyas?

Variegated hoyas, such as H. carnosa 'Variegata' with cream edges and the popular 'Krimson Queen' and 'Krimson Princess' types, need the same care with more light. The white and pink parts of the leaf do not photosynthesise, so in low light the plant grows slowly and new leaves come in greener. Pink tints on new leaves are stronger in bright light.

If a stem starts producing all-green leaves, cut it back to the last variegated leaf so the green growth does not take over.

## What pests and problems affect hoyas?

NC State lists mealybugs as the main pest, fungus gnats in mixes that stay wet, and spider mites in dry air. The Missouri Botanical Garden adds scale and warns that overwatering causes root rot. Check the leaf joints and the base of the flower spurs for white cottony clusters. Our guide to [common houseplant pests](/en/blog/common-houseplant-pests) explains how to treat each one.

Yellow leaves, leaf drop, wrinkled leaves and buds that fall before opening each have their own causes. Our article on [hoya problems](/en/blog/hoya-problems) goes through them symptom by symptom.

## Can a hoya go outside in summer?

Yes, in partial shade. NC State says a hoya can spend summer on a shaded patio and should come back inside when night temperatures fall below 10 °C (50°F). Check it for pests before bringing it in.

## Is a hoya safe for cats and dogs?

Yes. The [ASPCA](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/animal-poison-control/toxic-and-non-toxic-plants/wax-plant) lists Hoya carnosa as non-toxic to dogs and cats. Any plant eaten in quantity can still upset a pet's stomach. Our list of [plants safe for cats](/en/blog/plants-safe-for-cats) has more options.
