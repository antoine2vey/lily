Plant propagation cuttings are pieces of stem or leaf that grow roots and become a new plant, and most common houseplants root from one in 2 to 6 weeks. Take a stem 8 to 15 cm (3 to 6 in) long with several nodes, root it in water or moist potting mix in bright, indirect light, and pot it up once the roots are 3 to 5 cm (1 to 2 in) long. All you need is a clean blade, a jar or a small pot, and some patience.

## What you need

- Clean, sharp scissors or a knife. Wipe the blade with rubbing alcohol between plants.
- A glass jar, or a small pot with a drainage hole.
- Room-temperature water, or moist potting mix lightened with perlite.
- Optional: rooting hormone, and a clear plastic bag or dome to keep humidity in.
- A spot with bright, indirect light and no direct sun.

## Find the node first

A node is the slightly swollen joint on a stem where a leaf, a bud or an aerial root grows. Roots on a stem cutting form at the nodes, so every cutting needs at least one. A leaf on its own, cut from a vining plant like pothos, may grow roots but will never make a new stem.

[University of Vermont Extension](https://www.uvm.edu/extension/news/more-please-propagating-houseplants) recommends cuttings 3 to 6 inches (8 to 15 cm) long with several nodes. To take one:

1. Choose a healthy stem with no flowers.
2. Cut just below a node with a clean blade.
3. Strip the leaves from the lowest one or two nodes. Those nodes go under water or into the mix.
4. Keep a few leaves at the top so the cutting can photosynthesise.

Pruning and propagating are the same job, so the stems you cut back from a leggy plant make good cuttings. Our guide on [how to prune houseplants](/en/blog/how-to-prune-houseplants) shows where to cut.

## Rooting cuttings in water

Water lets you watch the roots form and suits most vining and soft-stemmed plants: pothos, heartleaf philodendron, tradescantia, coleus, begonia and mint.

1. Put the cutting in a jar of room-temperature water so the bare nodes are under water. UVM advises keeping the leaves above the waterline.
2. Place it in bright, indirect light. Direct sun heats the water and feeds algae.
3. Change the water every 5 to 7 days, and straight away if it turns cloudy.
4. Wait. UVM says rooting can take anywhere from days to months depending on the plant. Most common houseplants show roots within 2 to 6 weeks.

Pot the cutting up once the roots are 3 to 5 cm (1 to 2 in) long. UVM advises waiting until they are longer than 2 to 3 inches. Water roots are adapted to water, so keep the new pot evenly moist for the first 2 to 3 weeks while the plant grows soil roots, then let it dry between waterings as usual.

For monstera, which needs a node with an aerial root under water, see the section on [growing monstera in water](/en/blog/how-often-to-water-monstera).

## Rooting cuttings in soil

Rooting straight into soil skips the water-to-soil transition and suits woody and semi-woody plants, such as rosemary, ficus and fiddle-leaf fig, as well as succulents.

1. Fill a small pot with moist, free-draining mix, such as potting mix with extra perlite.
2. Dip the cut end in rooting hormone if you have it. UVM calls it optional for most houseplants.
3. Make a hole with a pencil so the hormone is not rubbed off, insert the cutting with its bare nodes below the surface, and firm the mix around it.
4. Cover the pot with a clear plastic bag or a dome to hold in humidity. Open it regularly so air can circulate and the cutting does not rot.
5. Keep the mix moist, not wet, and out of direct sun.
6. After 3 weeks, tug gently. UVM says resistance means roots have formed.

## Plant propagation cuttings, by plant

Not every houseplant roots from a stem. Use the method that matches the plant.

| Plant | Best method | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Pothos, heartleaf philodendron, tradescantia | Stem cutting in water | Among the easiest. See the [pothos care guide](/en/blog/pothos-care-guide) |
| Monstera | Stem cutting with a node and aerial root | Water or moist sphagnum moss |
| Rubber plant, fiddle-leaf fig | Stem cutting in soil | Rooting hormone helps |
| Succulents (jade, echeveria) | Stem or leaf cutting in dry mix | Let the cut end callus first |
| Snake plant | Leaf sections, or division | Leaf sections lose yellow edges |
| ZZ plant | Leaf cutting, or division | Slow to root |
| Calathea, peace lily | Division | No stem to cut from |

### Succulents

Let a succulent cutting or leaf dry for 24 to 48 hours so the cut end calluses over. A wet cut end rots. Then lay leaves on, or press stems into, dry cactus mix, and wait about a week before the first light watering.

### Snake plant

Cut a leaf into sections 5 to 7 cm (2 to 3 in) long and push each one upright into moist mix, keeping the same orientation it had on the plant. A section planted upside down will not root. Variegated varieties with yellow edges grow back plain green from leaf sections, so divide those instead. The [snake plant care guide](/en/blog/snake-plant-care-guide) covers division.

### Plants you divide instead

Calathea and peace lily grow as clumps from the base rather than on a stem with nodes. Propagate them by division: take the plant out of its pot, separate a section with its own roots and leaves, and pot it on its own. Spring repotting is the natural time, and the [spring repotting guide](/en/blog/spring-repotting-guide) walks through it.

## Rooting hormone

Rooting hormone contains a synthetic auxin, usually indole-3-butyric acid (IBA), that encourages roots to form at the cut. Most soft houseplants root without it. It helps more with woody stems and slow rooters.

- **Powder:** moisten the cut end, dip it, and tap off the excess.
- **Gel:** coat the cut end directly.

Only dip the amount you need into a separate dish, so you don't contaminate the whole container.

## Troubleshooting

- **The cutting wilts.** Normal in the first days, since it has no roots yet. Keep it covered and out of direct sun.
- **It rots in water.** The water is stale or the jar is dirty. Cut back to healthy tissue, wash the jar, and change the water more often.
- **It rots in soil.** The mix is too wet or the cover never comes off. Use a lighter mix and vent the bag daily.
- **No roots after 6 weeks.** Check that a node is under water or mix. Try a new cutting from younger growth, and use rooting hormone.
- **It dies after potting up.** The move from water to soil is a shock. Keep the mix evenly moist and the air humid for the first 2 to 3 weeks.

## When to take cuttings

Spring and early summer are best, when the parent plant is growing actively and cuttings root fastest. You can take cuttings all year, but in winter they root more slowly. [Lily](/en) can track each new pot as its own plant, with its own watering schedule, once it has roots.
